## A B S T R A C T

Using samples of Chinese listed companies from 2009 to 2023, this study examines the impact of media sentiment on corporate research and development (R&D) manipulation. Findings reveal companies' tendency to engage in upward (downward) R&D manipulation under positive (negative) media sentiment. Further analysis suggests that upward manipulation is motivated by a desire to cater to investor optimism fostered by positive media sentiment, while downward manipulation is motivated by a desire to alleviate performance pressures owing to negative media sentiment.

### 1. Introduction

Irrational behavior can cause a deviation between a company's market valuation and its actual value, thereby distorting its investment behavior (Dong et al., 2021). Although research and development (R&D) investment is crucial for technological competitiveness, its inherent risky attributes combined with low-market-efficiency conditions may distort resource allocation. Due to the inherent information asymmetry in R&D investment, managers manipulate R&D investments to accommodate market irrationality and seek personal gain (Zhang and Li, 2024). Furthermore, the high risk and long-term nature of R&D may cause management to be short-sighted, with focus only on immediate gains.

Media serves as a central conduit for information dissemination and has an increasingly significant influence on capital markets (Ahmed et al., 2024). However, media bias remains an issue (Yang et al., 2017; Fang et al., 2024). Media shapes corporate images and selectively discloses information; however, it can be manipulated to create positive narratives or sacrifice objectivity for sensation-alism (Bajo and Raimondo, 2017). Positive media narratives disproportionately underscore firms' growth opportunities, amplifying investor optimism and overconfidence (Tetlock, 2007). Meanwhile, media sentiment triggers emotional contagion. Investors, particularly retail participants with limited analytical capacity, often internalize the affective tone of media coverage, leading to herd behavior that reinforces sentiment persistence (Yang et al., 2017). Positive investor sentiment and herd behavior enhance buying pressures in capital markets, thus pushing stock prices beyond fundamental valuations. Hence, managers may create the illusion of "high R&D, high growth" to appeal to optimistic sentiments and maintain or elevate short-term stock prices (Liu et al., 2025). Moreover, during periods of positive media sentiment, analysts may focus on and thoroughly research market-favored companies, forming optimistic evaluations that attract more analyst attention and thereby boosting company visibility and investor sentiment (Hossain et al., 2024). In such cases, managers may be more motivated to engage in upward R&D manipulation to maintain investor optimism, resulting in actual R&D investments that exceed normal levels. Simultaneously, the information asymmetry of R&D investment incentivizes managers to manipulate for personal gain (Kothari et al., 2016; Baik et al., 2022). Negative media sentiment causes firms to face more pressure, pessimistic earnings forecasts, and shaken investor confidence, potentially triggering sell-offs and

stock price volatility and casting doubt on managerial capabilities (Yang et al., 2024). Under such performance pressure, managers may prefer stable short-term fixed asset investments over risky, capital-intensive R&D projects, leading to downward R&D manipulation in which actual R&D investment falls below the optimal level.

In recent years, Chinese enterprises' R&D investments have shown a surge trend driven by national strategies. R&D investment has become an important metric for enterprises to respond to policy guidance. Meanwhile, due to regulatory agencies' strict control over information disclosure by listed companies, the media has become an important channel for interpreting policies and corporate dynamics. However, unlike mature capital markets, individual investors make up a sizable proportion of market participants in China's capital market. Generally, these retail investors have characteristics such as insufficient financial literacy and weak information processing ability (Lee and Zhong, 2022), resulting in greater reliance on media coverage and susceptibility to the emotional influence of media narrative. Media's biased reporting, particularly in high-uncertainty R&D activities, may form an irrational market pricing mechanism via the transmission chain of "emotional contagion → cognitive bias → herd effect," leading to increased motivation for management to manipulate R&D investment based on short-term public opinion orientation (Zhang et al., 2022; Huynh et al., 2025). This demonstrates that the reality of China's capital market provides conditions for studying the impact of media sentiment on corporate R&D manipulation.

This study examines Chinese data and finds that media sentiment significantly influences R&D manipulation. Further analysis confirms that firms increase R&D expenditures under positive media sentiment to cater to investor optimism and decrease R&D investments to mitigate performance pressures when media sentiment is negative. It contributes to the literature in three ways. First, although previous research has primarily focused on the impact of market sentiment on corporate investment in inefficient capital markets (Polk and Sapienza, 2008; Arif and Lee, 2014; Hsu et al., 2024), this study examines the influence of media sentiment on corporate R&D investment, offering a novel theoretical lens for understanding heterogeneous R&D behaviors. Second, prior studies have primarily approached the motivations for corporate R&D manipulation from a policy rent-seeking perspective (Chen et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2022; Sun and Du, 2025). Meanwhile, this study places corporate R&D manipulation within a behavioral finance framework, investigating whether firms engage in R&D manipulation under the influence of media sentiment. Moreover, this study distinguishes itself by incorporating mediating mechanisms, providing a more nuanced understanding of the mechanisms through which media sentiment affects R&D investment decisions. Third, by examining the correlation between media sentiment and R&D manipulation, this study assists companies in identifying policies such as incentivizing transparent R&D disclosure to capture overinvestment and establishing a long-term performance evaluation system to optimize R&D investments and enhance corporate governance. The remainder of this study is organized as follows: Section 2 covers data and methodology. Section 3 presents the empirical results. Section 4 provides additional analysis. Section 5 concludes the study.

## 2. Data and methodology

### 2.1. Data

Considering data availability, this study's sample period spans from 2009 to 2023, excluding firms in the financial and real estate sectors. CSMAR is a reputable financial and economic database in China that has been extensively used in existing research. Datago database collects all news related to A-share listed companies from major newspapers and media outlets. Furthermore, it uses machine learning and natural language processing technology to conduct rigorous text analysis on raw news, resulting in more reliable sentiment results. Accordingly, we extract financial data from the CSMAR database and media sentiment data from the Datago database. Observations with missing values are excluded, and continuous variables are winsorized at 1 % and 99 %. Ultimately, the final sample comprises 14,993 firm-year observations.

#### 3.3. Robustness analysis

The original media sentiment index may be sensitive to weighting assumptions; therefore, following Piotroski et al. (2017), we reconstruct a weighted index (Media_sentiment_weight). To address potential sample selection bias caused by systematic differences and to equalize covariate distributions, we reweigh the sample using the entropy balancing method. We introduce cluster standard errors at the provincial level to account for spatial correlation in media sentiment and R&D activities within provinces. The 2015 market turbulence caused extreme investor sentiment swings, potentially distorting R&D decisions. Hence, we exclude samples from the 2015 Chinese stock market crash. Table A.2 presents the robustness analysis results. Across all tests, the media sentiment variable remains significantly positive.

### 4. Further analysis

Previous studies have shown that by adjusting R&D investments, management can achieve specific objectives. To examine the reasons behind various R&D manipulation behaviors, we construct  $ Up\_manipulation $ (when Abnormal RD > 0, take |Abnormal RD|; otherwise, take 0) and  $ Down\_manipulation $ (when Abnormal RD < 0, take |Abnormal RD|; otherwise, take 0), following Zhu et al. (2016).  $ Up\_manipulation $ and  $ Down\_manipulation $ indicate whether a company's actual R&D investment is greater and less than its normal R&D investment, respectively. Furthermore, based on previous analysis, we anticipate that the company will engage in R&D manipulation to cater to optimistic investors during periods of positive sentiment while alleviating performance pressure during periods of negative sentiment. Following Baker and Stein (2004) and Caskey and Ozel (2017), we further construct indicators for investor sentiment (Turn) and performance pressure (Press). The sample is divided into groups according to the median of these two indicators, respectively.

Table A.3 and Table A.4 present the results, showing that the interaction term between performance pressure and media sentiment is significantly negative. Moreover, the media sentiment coefficient of high-performance-pressure samples is significantly lower than that of low-performance samples, implying that addressing performance pressure is the primary motivator for negative R&D manipulation. The interaction term between investor and media sentiments is significantly positive, and the media sentiment coefficient of high-investor-sentiment samples is significantly higher than that of low-investor-sentiment samples, indicating that catering to market sentiment is the primary reason for upward R&D manipulation.

### 5. Conclusion

Using Chinese A-share listed companies as samples from 2009 to 2023, this study examines the relationship between media sentiment and corporate R&D manipulation. The empirical results reveal that when media sentiment is more positive, companies tend to engage in upward R&D manipulation by increasing R&D investment, while more negative media sentiment prompts companies to engage in downward R&D manipulation by reducing R&D investment. Further research suggests that the primary motivation for upward R&D manipulation is to cater to optimistic investor sentiment, which is fueled by positive media sentiment. Meanwhile, downward R&D manipulation is primarily intended to relieve performance pressures owing to negative media sentiment. Since mediadriven investor optimism leads to increased R&D overinvestment, regulatory bodies should require granular R&D disclosure standards to prevent window-dressing. Simultaneously, performance-based penalties may deter firms from making drastic R&D cuts during negative sentiment periods. To combat sentiment-driven manipulation, firms should implement dynamic R&D budget frameworks with multi-year performance benchmarks and establish independent oversight committees to audit R&D allocation decisions.
