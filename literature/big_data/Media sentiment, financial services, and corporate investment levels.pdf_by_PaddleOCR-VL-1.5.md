## A B S T R A C T

This study, based on data from A-share listed companies in China from 2008 to 2023, explores the relationship between media sentiment, financial services, and the level of corporate investment. The research findings indicate: first, that media sentiment has a significant positive impact on corporate investment levels; second, that financial services also significantly positively influence corporate investment levels; third, that financial services play a significant moderating role between media sentiment and corporate investment levels; and fourth, that there is substantial heterogeneity in the moderating effect of financial services on the relationship between media sentiment and corporate investment levels, particularly in firms experiencing high income growth and those with shorter listing durations, where the moderating effect of financial services is more pronounced. These results provide a new theoretical perspective for understanding the joint influence of media sentiment and financial services on corporate investment decision-making, and offer practical recommendations for policymakers.

### 1. Introduction

As global economic uncertainty continues to rise, enterprises face unprecedented challenges and opportunities in their investment decisions. Media sentiment, as an emerging external environmental factor, has gradually become an important variable influencing corporate investment level decisions. Research shows that media coverage affects the emotional fluctuations of investors, thereby influencing corporate investment behavior. Meanwhile, the degree of convenience in financial services is also one of the significant factors affecting corporate investment level decisions. The availability of financial services determines whether enterprises can effectively obtain the necessary funds, subsequently impacting their investment levels. Furthermore, a company's governance structure, such as board size and shareholder structure, also influences the decision-making process to a certain extent.

In recent years, the relationship between media sentiment, financial services, and corporate investment levels has become a focal point of interest in both academic and practical realms. Existing studies have shown that media sentiment has a significant impact on corporate investment levels (Fang et al., 2024). However, the moderating effect of financial services on corporate investment levels and the interaction between media sentiment and financial services have not been adequately explored (Hasselgren et al., 2022). Therefore, this study aims to fill this gap by exploring the relationship between media sentiment, financial services, and corporate investment levels, and further analyzing the moderating role of financial services in this relationship.

### 2. Literature review

#### 2.1. The impact of media sentiment on corporate investment levels

The influence of media sentiment as an external environmental factor on corporate investment decision-making has garnered wide attention in the academic community. Early research primarily focused on the impact of media coverage on investor sentiment. Studies have shown that media reports, by conveying information and emotions, influence investors' expectations about future economic conditions, thereby affecting their investment behavior (Wang & Zhu, 2024). For instance, negative sentiment may provoke uncertainty among investors, leading to a reduction in corporate investment activities; conversely, positive sentiment may stimulate investors' risk appetite and encourage firms to increase their investments (Dunham & Garcia, 2021).

Recent empirical research has further explored the specific effects of media sentiment on corporate investment levels. Several studies have found that media sentiment significantly influences corporate investment decisions, particularly in environments characterized by greater information asymmetry (Wang et al., 2025). During periods of high market volatility, fluctuations in media sentiment tend to exacerbate investors' emotional swings, thereby impacting corporate investment behavior (Li et al., 2023). Additionally, some scholars have pointed out that media sentiment affects companies of different sizes differently; larger enterprises may be better equipped to withstand the impacts of media sentiment, while small and medium-sized enterprises are more sensitive (Cevik et al., 2022).

#### 2.2. Financial services and corporate investment levels

The convenience of financial services significantly impacts corporate investment behavior. According to classical capital structure theory, a company's financing capacity and costs directly determine its investment level (Maxted, 2024). The availability of financial services helps enterprises reduce financing costs, thereby enhancing their investment capability. Existing research indicates a positive correlation between the availability of financial services and the level of corporate investment. Particularly during periods of loose financing conditions, companies can obtain more funds through bank loans, bond issuance, and other means, consequently increasing their investments (Famoti et al., 2024).

In addition to traditional financial services, the rise of digital finance and financial technology in recent years has also profoundly influenced corporate investment levels (Yiu et al., 2022). Digital finance provides companies with more financing channels by lowering financing costs and simplifying processes, particularly for small and medium-sized enterprises, where improvements in financial services have significantly boosted their investment capabilities (Keasey et al., 2025). However, some studies suggest that excessive convenience in financial services may lead to blind investments by businesses, particularly in environments characterized by severe information asymmetry, where companies may rely on overly optimistic media sentiment to drive investment while neglecting potential risks (Xu et al., 2022).

### 2.3. The interactive effect of media sentiment and financial services

Despite the achievements in individually studying the effects of media sentiment and financial services on corporate investment levels, research on the interactive effects between the two remains relatively scarce. Existing studies suggest that there may be interactions between media sentiment and financial services that jointly influence corporate investment decisions (Koratamaddi et al., 2021). Media sentiment can exacerbate or alleviate the influence of financial services on corporate investment levels by affecting investor confidence and decision-making. For instance, in scenarios with relatively positive media sentiment, investors may support increased corporate investment driven by optimism, even if firms face financing difficulties (Kyriazis et al., 2023). Conversely, negative sentiment can lead investors to be more sensitive to the financing risks of firms, thereby suppressing the positive impact of financial services on corporate investment levels (Xing, 2025).

Additionally, existing research has indicated that financial services can, to some extent, mitigate the negative effects of media sentiment on corporate investment levels (Bahoo et al., 2024). In a favorable financial services environment, firms can more effectively leverage capital market resources to offset the uncertainties induced by media sentiment (Gong et al., 2022). However, if the financial environment in which a firm operates is unstable, the negative effects of media sentiment may be amplified, leading firms to be more cautious in their investment decisions, or even to reduce their investments.

#### 2.4. Limitations of related research

Despite a considerable amount of research exploring the impact of media sentiment and financial services on corporate investment levels, existing literature still faces some limitations. On one hand, many studies primarily focus on the individual effects of media sentiment or financial services on corporate investment, with less emphasis on their interactions and the role that financial services play within this context. On the other hand, the empirical analyses in existing research mostly concentrate on isolated market environments, with inadequate attention to the heterogeneous responses of different types of firms, particularly regarding the significant differences that may exist between firms of varying income levels in terms of how media sentiment and financial services influence investment decisions.

### 3. Hypothesis proposal

Media sentiment plays a crucial signaling role in the modern economy, affecting investor emotions and decisions. Previous research has indicated that the emotional information conveyed by media reports can significantly influence corporate investors' confidence in the short term, thereby impacting their investment decisions (Islam et al., 2025). When media sentiment is predominantly positive, investors generally maintain an optimistic outlook on the future economy, reducing investment uncertainty and encouraging firms to increase their investments. Conversely, negative media sentiment may exacerbate market uncertainty, leading corporate investment decisions to become more cautious. Specifically, the convenience of financial services provides enterprises with more efficient channels for capital flow. For instance, when companies can obtain funding through a variety of financing channels such as bank loans, bond issuances, and equity financing, they can optimize their capital structure without relying on a single source of funding, thereby achieving more flexible and sustainable investment decisions. This availability of funding reduces the financing constraints faced by companies, decreasing the likelihood of investment slowdowns or stagnation due to insufficient funds. Furthermore, the improvement of financial services can also directly affect corporate capital expenditures by lowering financing costs. In countries and regions with mature capital markets and well-established financial systems, companies can swiftly secure financing support, often leading to more proactive investment decisions, especially in an environment characterized by high economic uncertainty, where the role of financial services is particularly evident. Conversely, in areas with relatively underdeveloped financial systems and higher capital costs, companies experience increased difficulty in securing financing, significantly suppressing investment activities. Therefore, this paper proposes the following hypothesis.

##### Hypothesis 1. Media sentiment has a significant positive effect on corporate investment levels.

The convenience and accessibility of financial services play a crucial role in the investment decisions of enterprises (Liu et al., 2021). According to capital structure theory, a firm's financing capability directly impacts its capital allocation and investment decisions. When an enterprise can secure sufficient and lower-cost funding, its investment activities are more likely to be expanded and reinforced (Zhang et al., 2022). Improving financial services, particularly in terms of diversifying financing channels and reducing financing costs, can significantly enhance a company's investment capacity, encouraging greater investment in production equipment, research and development innovations, market expansion, and other areas. Therefore, this paper proposes the following hypothesis.

#### Hypothesis 2. Financial services have a significant positive impact on the investment levels of enterprises.

In the modern economic environment, media sentiment, as an external source of information, can significantly influence investor sentiment and market expectations. Particularly during periods of high economic uncertainty or substantial market volatility, fluctuations in media sentiment may exacerbate investor emotional swings, thereby affecting corporate investment decisions (Hsu et al., 2024). However, the availability of financial services may play a alleviating role amid such emotional fluctuations, especially when negative media sentiment has a considerable impact, as enterprises can secure funding through effective financial service channels, thereby stabilizing their investment levels (Sun et al., 2024).

When media sentiment skews negative, investors may develop pessimistic expectations about the future economic situation, which can lead to instability in the capital markets, increased difficulty for businesses in obtaining financing, and restricted investment activities. However, in an environment with well-developed financial services, businesses can more easily secure financing support (Chai et al., 2025). The convenience of financial services reduces the costs and risks associated with obtaining funds, allowing companies to still access necessary financial support and maintain their investment levels, even under the impact of negative media sentiment. Specifically, financial services can not only provide funding support but also enhance a company's resilience against risks, enabling it to continue making long-term strategic investments under external pressures (Pineiro-Chousa et al., 2022). Thus, this paper proposes the following hypothesis.

#### Hypothesis 3. Financial services moderate the relationship between media sentiment and the level of corporate investment

Businesses with different levels of income growth may exhibit varying responses when faced with media sentiment and financial services. Companies with high income growth levels typically possess more stable funding sources and stronger market competitiveness. Therefore, even in the face of negative media sentiment, these companies may overcome external pressures through their inherent advantages, maintaining a high level of investment (Cao & Huang, 2025). In contrast, businesses with low income growth levels often encounter more complex funding and financing issues. In light of the uncertainties posed by media sentiment, the role of financial services may be even more critical for them. High income growth companies may exhibit a lower dependence on financial services, while those with low income growth levels are more reliant on effective financial service support to mitigate the uncertainties arising from media sentiment (Long et al., 2023). Consequently, this paper proposes the following hypothesis.

## Hypothesis 4. The moderating effect of financial services on the relationship between media sentiment and corporate investment levels exhibits heterogeneity between firms with high and low income growth levels.

First, companies with a longer listing history typically possess a more mature capital structure and a higher level of market recognition, which allows for a more diversified array of financing options and relatively easier access to financial services. For these companies, the influence of media sentiment on investment decisions may be relatively subdued, as they can offset short-term fluctuations in the external environment through stable capital sources. In this context, the role of financial services may be more about reducing financing costs and enhancing the efficiency of funds utilization, thereby optimizing investment decisions (Han & Gu, 2021).

In contrast, companies with a shorter listing history often face greater uncertainty and issues related to information asymmetry,

making fluctuations in media sentiment likely to have a more pronounced effect on their investment decisions. Given the limited financing channels and support from capital markets for these companies, they may rely more heavily on financial services to mitigate funding costs and risks in the face of negative media sentiment, thereby maintaining normal investment levels. Thus, for companies with shorter listing histories, financial services play a more critical moderating role in alleviating the negative impact of media sentiment.

From a theoretical perspective, a company's market positioning, brand effect, and financing history can influence its investment decisions in different market environments. Companies with longer listing histories possess stronger capital allocation capabilities and risk resilience, whereas those with shorter listing histories need to depend more on financial services to navigate external uncertainties. Consequently, this paper proposes the following hypothesis.

Hypothesis 5. The moderating effect of financial services on the relationship between media sentiment and corporate investment levels varies between companies with longer and shorter listing histories.

### 4. Research design

### 4.1. Data sources and sample selection

This study focuses on A-share listed companies in China from 2008 to 2023. Based on the annual reports of these listed companies and other publicly available information, a panel dataset comprising 35,473 company-year observations has been constructed. The sample includes companies from multiple industries and regions, providing strong representativeness. The primary data sources are the CSMAR database and the CNRDS database, which are prevalent databases in China-related studies (D. Kong et al., 2025; G. Kong et al., 2025).

### 4.2. Variable definition and measurement

The key variables in this study include media sentiment, financial services, and corporate investment levels. Their specific definitions are as follows.

#### (1) Corporate investment level (invest)

This indicator serves as the dependent variable in this study. It is measured by the ratio of cash paid for fixed assets, intangible assets, and other long-term assets to total assets at the beginning of the period. This metric reflects the intensity and scale of corporate investment activities.

#### (2) Media sentiment (mediasen)

This indicator functions as the independent variable in this study. Media sentiment analysis is conducted using natural language processing techniques (NLP) to derive a media sentiment index from media texts. A higher index value indicates a more positive media sentiment.

#### 4.3. Model specification

To validate the hypotheses, this paper constructs the following basic regression model:

 $$ invest_{i,t}=\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}Finratio1_{i,t}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{k}C_{i,t}+\mu_{i}+\pi_{t}+\varepsilon_{i,t} $$ 

 $$ invest_{i,t}=\varpi_{0}+\varpi_{1}mediasen_{i,t}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\varpi_{k}C_{i,t}+\mu_{i}+\pi_{t}+\varepsilon_{i,t} $$ 

In this context, invest refers to the investment level of enterprises, mediasen denotes media sentiment, and Finratio1 indicates the facilitation level of financial services. C represents a set of control variables, while  $ \mu_i $ and  $ \pi_t $ are the individual fixed effects and year fixed effects, respectively, with  $ \varepsilon_{i,t} $ being the error term.

To explore the moderating effect of financial services on the relationship between media sentiment and enterprise investment levels, this study further introduces the interaction term between media sentiment and financial services.

 $$ invest_{i,t}=\beta_{0}+\beta_{1}Finratio1_{i,t}+\beta_{2}mediasen_{i,t}+\beta_{3}Finratio1_{i,t}*mediasen_{i,t}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\beta_{k}C_{i,t}+\mu_{i}+\pi_{t}+\varepsilon_{i,t} $$ 

Among them, Finratio1*mediasen represents the interaction term between media sentiment and financial services, used to examine the moderating effect of financial services on the relationship between media sentiment and corporate investment levels.

### 4.4. Empirical methods

This study employs panel data regression analysis, utilizing a fixed effects model (FE) that incorporates individual fixed effects and year fixed effects, effectively eliminating the influence of unobserved individual characteristics and time trends on the results. Additionally, this paper conducts tests for endogeneity, using financial market volatility (finwave) as an instrumental variable to further verify the robustness of the regression results.

Table 8 presents the results of the moderating heterogeneity analysis. This study classifies enterprises into two groups, high income growth enterprises and low income growth enterprises, based on the median income growth level. In Column (1), for low income growth enterprises, the coefficient for the interaction term between media sentiment (mediasen) and financial services (Finratio1), represented as (mediasen*Finratio1), is 0.0053 and does not reach a significant level. In Column (2), for high income growth enterprises, the coefficient for the interaction term (mediasen*Finratio1) is 0.2353, which is significant at the 1% level, indicating that in high income growth enterprises, financial services play a substantial moderating role in the relationship between media sentiment and the level of corporate investment.

Overall, these results suggest that the moderating effect of financial services on the relationship between media sentiment and corporate investment levels exhibits significant heterogeneous effects across enterprises with different income growth levels, thereby validating Hypothesis 4.

#### (2) Heterogeneity of listing duration

Based on the results of the moderated heterogeneity analysis presented in Table 9, it can be observed that the moderating effect of financial services on the relationship between media sentiment and corporate investment levels exhibits significant heterogeneity across companies with varying listing durations. For firms with longer listing durations, the coefficient of the interaction term of media sentiment and financial ratio (mediasen*Finratio1) is 0.0041, which is significant at the 1 % level, indicating a positive moderating role of financial services in the process by which media sentiment influences corporate investment levels. Conversely, for firms with shorter listing durations, the coefficient of the interaction term is 0.3049, which is also significant at the 1 % level. This result suggests that, compared to firms with longer listing durations, financial services exert a stronger moderating effect on the relationship between media sentiment and corporate investment levels in companies with shorter listing durations. Thus, Hypothesis 5 is validated.

### 6. Research conclusions and policy recommendations

#### 6.1. Research conclusions

This study empirically analyzes the relationship among media sentiment, financial services, and corporate investment levels. The findings reveal that both media sentiment and financial services have a significant positive impact on corporate investment levels. Moreover, financial services play a significant moderating role between media sentiment and corporate investment levels. There are notable differences in this moderating effect across companies with varying income growth levels and years since listing; specifically, the effect is more pronounced in companies with high income growth and shorter listing durations compared to those with low income growth and longer listing histories. These results provide new insights into how media sentiment and financial services jointly influence corporate investment decision-making.

### 6.2. Policy recommendations

Based on the research conclusions, this paper puts forward the following policy recommendations: First, both the government and corporations should pay attention to changes in media sentiment and actively guide media reporting, especially during economic fluctuations, to prevent the excessive spread of negative sentiment and reduce investor panic. Second, the government should further optimize the financial service environment, particularly in terms of diversifying financing channels and reducing financing costs, to enhance the accessibility of financing for enterprises and promote higher levels of investment. Finally, for companies with different income growth levels, policies should be tailored to the specific circumstances of these companies, implementing differentiated financial support measures to strengthen the financing capabilities of low-income growth enterprises and facilitate their steady development.
