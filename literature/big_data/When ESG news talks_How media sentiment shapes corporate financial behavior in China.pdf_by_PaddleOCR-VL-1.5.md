## ABSTRACT

News sentiment affects company practices. Using Baidu News reports spanning 2007–2022 on Chinese A-share listed companies as text data and machine learning and text analysis methods, this study measures environmental, social, and governance (ESG) news sentiment indices. This study examines the impact of ESG news sentiment on corporate financial asset allocation. Findings reveal that optimistic ESG news sentiment has a significant negative impact on corporate financial asset allocation. Mechanism analysis indicates that ESG news sentiment can restrain corporate financialization by facilitating corporate access to ESG-related financial support, reducing operational risks, and promoting real investments, which weakens risk aversion and profit-seeking motives. Further analysis reveals that the financialization governance function of ESG news sentiment is more prominent for private enterprises, during nonrecession periods, and for heavily polluting enterprises. Moreover, it is significant in regions with superior digital financial development and higher ESG governance intensity. From the perspective of ESG news content and information, the environmental and governance dimensions of news sentiment and neutral ESG news attention exhibit stronger financialization suppression effects. This study provides a new perspective for addressing financialization concerns and demonstrates the supervisory influence of the media on corporate sustainability.

## 1. Introduction

In emerging economies, traditional financial systems face the persistent challenge of increasing corporate financialization; therefore, exploring ways to promote the deep integration of the financial system with the real economy is an important topic. Corporate financialization refers to the growing tendency of nonfinancial firms to prioritize financial activities as a profit source, investment choice, and asset allocation structure (Krippner, 2005). Data indicate that since 2010, Chinese-listed companies have reduced physical investments, while the proportion of financial assets has steadily increased. This trend not only crowds out investments in core business areas but also hinders productivity and performance improvements (Demir, 2009). Therefore, exploring effective governance mechanisms for advancing corporate financialization is essential for regulating corporate investment and financing practices, preventing systemic financial risks, and maintaining financial markets' stability (Riccetti et al., 2016).

In recent years, the concept of corporate environmental, social, and governance (ESG) performance has gained increasing recognition in China's capital market. A growing number of listed companies are actively engaged in ESG practices such as green transformation, social responsibility initiatives, and governance innovation. Financial institutions have also begun incorporating ESG performance as a key criterion in investment decisions, offering higher valuations and improved financing access to firms with strong ESG performance $ ^{2} $ (Chen et al., 2023; Feng et al., 2022). However, the rising trend of corporate financialization fundamentally contradicts the principles of ESG. From a developmental perspective, ESG emphasizes long-term sustainable growth, urging firms to allocate more resources to core business operations that generate lasting value. From a value creation standpoint, ESG is fundamentally a stakeholder-oriented model that balances the interests of shareholders, employees, customers, communities, and the environment (Huang, 2022). In contrast, corporate financialization prioritizes short-term financial returns, often engaging in capital arbitrage to maximize the interests of shareholders or executives—actions that frequently conflict with the values and needs of broader societal stakeholders such as governments, communities, and consumers. Therefore, as ESG principles continue to be widely disseminated and adopted, corporate financialization, which is fundamentally incompatible with ESG objectives, is expected to face increasing constraints.

News sentiment has a constructive influence on promoting the dissemination and adoption of ESG principles. ESG news sentiment refers to the overall emotional tone reflected in media coverage of a company's ESG performance. It serves as a key signal for capital markets to evaluate a firm's ESG standing and an important external force that guides corporate practices toward sustainable development (Yu et al., 2023). As such, ESG news sentiment is expected to have a positive governance effect by reducing firms' tendency toward financialization and encouraging greater investment in the real economy. This expectation is based on the understanding that corporate financialization is primarily driven by two motivations, the first of which is risk-avoidance. Firms that face constraints in accessing external capital may choose to allocate resources to financial assets to preserve liquidity and reduce the risk of future funding shortfalls (Demir, 2009). The second is the profit-seeking motive, wherein, under the influence of self-interest or performance pressure, managers may prefer investing in short-term, low-risk, and high-return financial instruments that can crowd out or marginalize investment in core production activities (Orhangazi, 2008).

ESG news sentiment may mitigate these motivations through two distinct mechanisms. From the perspective of capital acquisition, positive ESG-related media coverage sends favorable signals to the market, indicating that the firm has sound internal governance and a strong commitment to sustainable development. In capital markets characterized by information asymmetry, such signals imply that the firm is exposed to lower traditional financial risk such as agency conflicts and stock price crash risk and emerging ESG-related risks such as regulatory sanctions or consumer boycotts triggered by negative ESG publicity (Chasiotis et al., 2024). As a result, investors and creditors, particularly those in the ESG investment space, are more likely to assume that firms with strong positive ESG reputations have better repayment capacity and long-term growth prospects. This perception increases the likelihood that such firms will gain access to external financing, $ ^{3} $ reducing the need to hold financial assets as a precautionary buffer against liquidity risk. From the perspective of capital utilization, favorable ESG media sentiment can enhance a company's reputational capital while also increasing the likelihood that any actions that harm stakeholder interests will be publicly exposed. To protect their reputations and remain consistent with stated ESG strategies (Yu et al., 2023), corporate managers are more likely to revise any financial practices that conflict with ESG objectives. Specifically, they may reduce short-term profit-driven investments in financial assets and instead allocate more resources to core business operations and long-term projects that align with the interests of a broader set of stakeholders, significantly constraining the financialization tendency driven by speculative motives.

This study examines how changes in media ESG sentiment influence corporate financial market practices. This exploration has academic value and provides valuable insights for businesses' and investors' practical decision-making. We expect our findings to provide new theoretical support for understanding the media's influence in the contemporary economy and offer practical advice for promoting sustainable investment. We choose ESG news sentiment as a measure rather than general media sentiment or ESG institutional scores for two reasons. First, ESG news avoids the lack of specificity and professionalism that can result from indiscriminately examining all media reports, particularly those from nonprofessional sources such as social media. Second, significant discrepancies exist in third-party ESG rating agencies' data (Berg et al., 2022; Chatterji et al., 2016), which may also be affected by corporate "greenwashing." ESG news sentiment offers strong objectivity, stable continuity, and a large volume of data, enabling this measure to accurately and scientifically portray the media's view of a company's ESG performance and capture the impact of ESG media focus on corporate decision-making.

To investigate the impact of media ESG sentiment on corporate financial asset allocation practices, we use Chinese enterprises as the research object for two main reasons. First, according to the Global Sustainable Investment Alliance report, $ ^{4} $ the Asia–Pacific region witnesses rapid growth in ESG investment, particularly in China. This context makes Chinese enterprises’ financial asset allocation

practices driven by ESG sentiment a significant area of study, reflecting the sustainable development challenges faced by many emerging economies. Second, institutional investors in China increasingly prioritize ESG factors, with investors generally associating ESG issues with financial performance (Friede et al., 2015). Moreover, internet development in China enhances the impact of media public opinion oversight. Therefore, exploring the influence of media ESG sentiment as an external factor on Chinese enterprises' financial asset allocation strategies can reveal how cultural and market environment differences can result in varying financial responses in similar ESG external contexts, providing a foundation for international theoretical development.

We investigate the impact and mechanism of ESG news sentiment on corporate financialization based on signal and principal-agent theories (Banker et al., 2000; Fama & Jensen, 1983; Huang, 2022; Jensen & Meckling, 2019). Our findings reveal that positive ESG news sentiment effectively suppresses corporate financialization, and this conclusion holds even following a series of robustness tests. Mechanism tests reveal that positive ESG news sentiment can reduce corporate financialization by easing financing constraints and lowering operational risks, which weakens the risk-averse motivations for financialization. ESG news sentiment can also enhance corporate physical investment scale and reduce financing costs, weakening the profit-driven motivations for financialization. Further analysis demonstrates that the positive effects of ESG news sentiment are more pronounced for private enterprises, during non-recession periods, for heavily polluting companies, and in regions with better digital financial development and higher ESG governance intensity. Moreover, from the perspective of news content and information, reports that focus on environmental and governance dimensions and neutral ESG news inhibit financialization more significantly.

Our research contributes to the existing literature in three key areas. First, previous research has primarily examined overall media sentiment (Dyck et al., 2008; You et al., 2018) without distinguishing between ESG news and regular news. However, significant differences exist between ESG news and regular news, and their impacts on corporate decision-making are also distinct. Treating these news forms the same could result in biased effect identification. This study innovatively addresses this gap, using Python to scrape 2,276,364 ESG news articles from Baidu News. We classify these articles using the Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) topic model and perform sentiment analysis on the text content using machine learning algorithms with SnowNLP for natural language processing. This innovative approach enables us to construct an ESG news sentiment index, which provides unique information that third-party ESG rating indicators cannot capture, expanding valuable data sources for research on corporate finance practices.

Second, existing literature has primarily focused on the impact of corporate social responsibility (CSR)/ESG ratings (Feng et al., 2022) on corporate performance (Chen et al., 2023). Some recent studies have started to examine the influence of ESG news reports on corporate capital market performance (Chen et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2023); however, these studies have overlooked the potential emotions and preferences conveyed in ESG news, making it difficult to reveal its true impact on corporate financial decision-making. To the best of our knowledge, this study is the first to explore the influence of ESG news sentiment on corporate financialization from this perspective, examining it within the context of sustainable development conflicts. Our findings provide the latest evidence regarding how media transmission mechanisms can promote corporate sustainability.

Third, in contrast to existing literature that has analyzed the inhibitory effects of corporate financialization from financial regulation (Yang et al., 2023) and corporate organizational structure optimization (Li et al., 2023) perspectives, our study demonstrates the internal mechanisms by which ESG news sentiment suppresses corporate financialization by weakening risk-averse and speculative profit-seeking motivations. We also conduct an in-depth analysis of the differences in this impact across various enterprises, industries, and regions, as well as the content and information density of news articles. This enriches research on the factors influencing corporate financialization and provides new insights and market tools for governing economic financialization in emerging economies.

We arranged the paper's structure as follows. Section 2 presents a review of the existing literature, followed by the proposal of theoretical hypotheses in Section 3. We introduce the research methods and data in Section 4. Finally, we analyze the empirical results in Section 5 and present our conclusions and recommendations in Section 6.

## 2. Literature review

### 2.1. Media's impact on corporations

Media reporting is a crucial means of creating and disseminating information, and its impact on corporations has received significant research attention. These effects can be broadly categorized into three main considerations. First, from the information transmission perspective, media reports can be an effective supplement to formal corporate disclosures. They can motivate companies to actively increase information disclosure to advance organizational legitimacy (Aerts & Cormier, 2009), reducing information asymmetry between companies and stakeholders (Kutsuna et al., 2009). For example, media attention can alleviate information friction in capital markets, improving stock pricing accuracy (Fang & Peress, 2009). Second, from the agency problems perspective, media coverage can accelerate the exposure of corporate misconduct and attract regulatory attention, increasing the likelihood of violations being discovered and resolved (Dyck et al., 2008). Media coverage can also regulate management behavior by influencing management reputations (Fama & Jensen, 1983), compensation, and even executive turnover (You et al., 2018), reducing the likelihood of agency problems. Third, companies can sometimes manipulate the media to serve management's interests. For example, companies might engage in media opinion buying or release press statements to influence media sentiment to inflate IPO valuations (Bajo & Raimondo, 2017) or conceal corporate misconduct (Zavyalova et al., 2012).

### 2.2. ESG'S impact on corporate development

Neoclassical economic theory suggests that the strong externalities of ESG investment do not contribute to maximizing corporate

value and cannot generate adequate financial returns (Bénabou & Tirole, 2010). Friede et al. (2015) examined the literature on the relationship between corporate ESG performance and financial performance, finding that the majority of studies demonstrated a negative correlation between the two. However, recent research has increasingly recognized the positive impact of strong ESG performance on corporate development. ESG investments facilitate the communication of long-term business objectives and signal a commitment to stakeholder responsibility (Flammer, 2018; Huang, 2022), which alleviates information asymmetry between companies and external stakeholders. Furthermore, ESG investments can reduce managerial opportunism and external market risks through insurance effects and incentivized information disclosure (Godfrey et al., 2009; Rezaee & Tuo, 2019). Consequently, numerous studies have confirmed that ESG investment positively contributes to reducing corporate financing costs (Chen et al., 2023; Pástor et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2024), enhances investment efficiency (Benlemlih & Bitar, 2018), and improves product market recognition (Bardos et al., 2020).

### 2.3. Causes of corporate financialization

Financialization refers to corporations' increasing focus on capital profits over supporting core production activities (Froud et al., 2000). The most prominent characteristic of this trend is the continuous increase in corporate financial asset allocations (Stockhammer, 2004). There are three main explanations for corporate financialization. First, it acts as a liquidity management tool. The ability to rapidly liquidate financial assets enables companies facing financing constraints to maintain liquidity reserves, which mitigates the risks of future capital shortages from policy changes or external shocks (Almeida et al., 2004; Hsieh & Klenow, 2009). Second, a return differential exists between financial and real investments. When the cost of financing real investments is high or real estate and stock market bubbles increase the returns on financial assets, companies tend to hold more financial assets (Demir, 2009). Third, financialization can have a mediating financial influence. Some companies with financing advantages in formal financial systems use shadow banking to relend funds obtained from financial markets to credit-constrained firms, earning a spread in the process, which also increases financialization (Du et al., 2017). Financialization can crowd out real investments and exacerbate financial market risks (Stockhammer, 2004) and hinder corporate sustainable development. In summary, while some research has focused on the impact of ESG news sentiment on stock prices (Yu et al., 2023), limited exploration of the corporate governance functions of ESG media sentiment remains. Additionally, most measures of media sentiment have relied on manually coding news as positive or negative and calculating the proportion of such reports (Dyck et al., 2008; You et al., 2018), which cannot capture the nuanced sentiment tendencies in media coverage and introduces potential issues of bias and insufficient data. Furthermore, although the causes of corporate financialization have been extensively examined, the majority of research has focused on external regulatory policies or internal organizational improvements (Li et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2023), with insufficient attention to the influence of media sentiment on this process.

## 3. Hypotheses development

Optimistic ESG news sentiment can have a critical influence on reducing information asymmetry between companies and stakeholders. This may enhance firms' ability to access external financing, particularly in the form of ESG-related financial instruments, weakening the motivation to engage in financialization as a precautionary response to risk. In recent years, China's continuous promotion of carbon peak and neutrality goals, combined with frequent incidents involving corporate violations of consumer and labor rights, has drawn increasing attention from investors and creditors to the ESG risks and opportunities in enterprises' portfolios. $ ^{5} $ As a result, ESG financial products such as green finance instruments, ESG mutual funds, and ESG loans have experienced rapid development. $ ^{6} $ These ESG financial instruments provide firms with longer maturity periods, greater flexibility, and lower financing costs, which alleviate financing constraints. However, according to a 2023 Report on ESG Investment Development in China's Asset Management Industry that was jointly released by Huaxia Wealth Management and The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen, one of the primary challenges for financial institutions in conducting ESG investments is the lack of standardized ESG disclosure rules, limited accessibility of ESG-related information, and concerns regarding the completeness and reliability of the disclosed data. $ ^{7} $ Zhang et al. (2023) also emphasized that ESG disclosure in China remains relatively low compared with more mature ESG investment markets. In this context, improved transparency in firms' ESG performance, particularly through positive ESG news sentiment, may significantly

enhance the likelihood that firms will obtain support from ESG-focused financial resources. This outcome can be explained from the perspective of ESG loan financing wherein firms with stronger ESG performance are more likely to meet the eligibility criteria for credit products such as green loans and ESG-linked financing (Li et al., 2024). In line with China's green finance regulatory requirements, $ ^{8} $ financial institutions must conduct comprehensive assessments of borrowing firms' ESG risks and repayment capacity. Since 2012, the Chinese government has implemented a series of regulations to guide this process. The 2012 Green Credit Guidelines required banks to identify and manage environmental and social risks within their lending activities. These efforts were expanded in 2022 through the Green Finance Guidelines for the Banking and Insurance Industries, which introduced a broader risk identification framework encompassing ESG factors, and required financial institutions to incorporate ESG elements into overall risk management systems. In this regulatory context, consistently positive ESG news coverage can serve as a credible external signal that lenders use to assess whether a firm meets ESG loan conditions. Firms with a higher volume of favorable ESG reporting tend to exhibit lower ESG-related risks such as environmental liabilities, ethical controversies, regulatory penalties, and stock price volatility, all of which directly influence presumed repayment capacity (Chen & Xie, 2022). These forward-looking risks are often difficult to detect using traditional financial statements, particularly given the limited scope of ESG disclosure among Chinese firms. Therefore, a strong record of positive ESG media sentiment can improve a firm's chances of qualifying for ESG loan support.

From the perspective of ESG equity financing, long-term institutional investors such as ESG mutual funds place greater emphasis on evaluating and screening investment targets' nonfinancial performance when constructing portfolios. These investors tend to exclude so-called "sin stocks" with poor ESG performance (Zhang et al., 2023). However, inconsistent ESG ratings and the incomplete nature of corporate ESG disclosures have made media coverage an important supplementary channel through which ESG investors can determine a firm's actual ESG practices (Chen et al., 2024). Firms that receive sustained positive ESG coverage are more likely to be included in ESG fund portfolios and gain access to long-term equity financing support. In addition, ESG financial investors monitor firms' use of funds by stipulating earmarked use clauses, submitting shareholder proposals, or exercising voting rights to ensure that capital is directed toward sustainability-related objectives (Dimson et al., 2015). Such strict conditions on capital use have a constructive influence on ensuring that financing is allocated to firms' real-sector operations rather than diverted to shadow banking activities that may intensify financialization and run counter to ESG principles. Therefore, positive sentiment in ESG-related news can help firms secure ESG financial resources and ease financing constraints, reducing the tendency to accumulate liquid financial assets purely for risk-hedging purposes, which is a key manifestation of financialization (Almeida et al., 2004; Hsieh & Klenow, 2009). Therefore, we propose the following research hypothesis.

### H1. Positive ESG news sentiment suppresses corporate financialization by assisting companies' access to ESG financial support.

Agency theory highlights how the separation of ownership and control can lead to managerial myopia, which affects long-term corporate objectives and shareholder interests (Jensen & Meckling, 2019). Motivated by short-term goals to maintain operational performance and stock prices, management may prioritize financial asset investments that offer immediate returns, lower costs, and reduced risks, sidelining real investments and fostering corporate financialization (Orhangazi, 2008). However, positive ESG news sentiment can incentivize management to pursue investments that support long-term sustainable corporate growth, which is evident in two key areas. First, from a monitoring perspective, media coverage has a significant influence on improving internal corporate governance and mitigating agency problems (Dyck et al., 2008). Positive ESG news builds firms' social reputation capital and can influence managers' personal reputations, compensation, and career prospects; however, it also increases the reputational costs associated with violating sustainability principles (Yu et al., 2023). Since the media tends to focus on sensational events to attract a broader audience (Hermida et al., 2012), firms that engage in inconsistent ESG practices are more likely to attract media attention and scrutiny, which can result in sanctions from stakeholders. To avoid negative publicity, protect reputational capital, and safeguard their own professional trajectories, corporate managers are incentivized to strengthen internal governance and reduce short-term financial arbitrage practices that contradict sustainability goals. Second, from an incentive perspective, according to catering theory (Baker & Wurgler, 2004), managers may actively pursue real investment projects that align with investors' positive expectations regarding ESG responsibility, influenced by media sentiment. This alignment can foster sustainable corporate development through initiatives such as digitalization or green innovations. Furthermore, signaling theory suggests that consistent and credible signals enhance trust and reliability (Spence, 1973). Positive evaluations of a company's ESG performance can signal the stability of operational income supporting ESG investments to the external environment (Huang, 2022) and management's commitment to long-term corporate objectives (Banker et al., 2000). These signals can mitigate information asymmetry among stakeholders, enhance compliance in fund allocation for real economic activities, reduce the risk premium associated with information asymmetry (Handa et al., 1993), and facilitate external funding support for corporate real investments. Consequently, with increased incentives and resources for management to invest in long-term industrial development, the inclination toward financialization in corporate asset allocation for short-term profit-driven ventures is naturally subdued. Therefore, we propose the following research hypothesis.

H2 Positive ESG news sentiment can alleviate agency problems by motivating managers to increase tangible investments, restraining corporate financialization.

## 4. Methodology and data

### 4.1. Model setting

To investigate the impact of ESG news sentiment on corporate financialization, we established the following model:

 $$ Finance_{it}=\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}ESG_{i,t-1}+\alpha_{2}\mathbf{X}_{i,t-1}+\delta_{i}+\theta_{jt}+\varepsilon_{kt}+\mu_{it} $$ 

where  $ Finance_{it} $ represents company i's financial asset allocation in year t.  $ ESG_{i,t-1} $ denotes company i's ESG news media sentiment in year t-1.  $ X_{i,t-1} $ is a set of control variables at the enterprise level in year t-1.  $ \delta_{i} $,  $ \theta_{jt} $, and  $ \varepsilon_{kt} $ refers to company, industry-year, and province-year fixed effects (FEs), respectively, and  $ \mu_{it} $ is the random error term.

### 4.2. Variable selection

#### 4.2.1. Explained variable: corporate financialization (finance)

Stockhammer (2004) defined corporate financialization as a phenomenon in which nonfinancial firms increase leverage, enhance stock dividends, and engage in financial markets more actively. This financialization can be driven by motivations to hedge risks and maintain liquidity reserves (Hsieh & Klenow, 2009) or by speculative motives such as investing in financial assets or engaging in intermediary financial activities (Demir, 2009; Du et al., 2017). Regardless of the motivation, financialization is characterized by a continuous increase in financial asset allocation (Stockhammer, 2004). According to Demir (2009) and Duchin et al. (2017), financial assets include traded assets, net financial assets available for sale, held-to-maturity investments, long-term equity investments, derivative financial assets, loans and advances, and receivable dividends and interest. Therefore, this study primarily examines corporate financialization tendencies from the perspective of asset allocation structures by measuring the proportion of financial assets to total assets (Li & Shen, 2023).

#### 4.2.2. Explanatory variable: ESG news sentiment (ESG)

We measure ESG news sentiment (ESG) by the average sentiment value of ESG news texts for each company in a given year. ESG is a continuous and comparable value ranging from 0 to 1, where a score closer to 1 indicates a more positive overall tone in ESG-related news reports about the firm during the year, while a score closer to 0 indicates a more negative tone. The calculation steps are as follows. First, we used Python to scrape relevant news articles about publicly listed companies from the Baidu News website, $ ^{9} $ supplemented by manual searches and corrections. Second, after cleaning the news text data, we applied company relevance determination and the LDA topic model to identify whether news articles were related to listed companies' ESG topics. We categorized these topics into environmental (E), social (S), and governance (G) dimensions. We collected 2,276,364 ESG news articles related to listed companies from 2007 to 2022. Finally, we conducted sentiment analysis on the news content using the SnowNLP algorithm. $ ^{10} $ After obtaining sentiment scores for news texts ranging from 0 to 1 (with scores closer to 0 indicating more negative sentiment and those closer to 1 indicating more positive sentiment). We then calculated the company-level ESG news sentiment index based on the annual average distribution. $ ^{11} $ This index effectively mitigates issues related to discrepancies and poor comparability in third-party ESG ratings and corporate greenwashing practices (Flammer, 2021).

#### 4.2.3. Control variables

Following common practices in existing literature (Naumer & Yurtoglu, 2022; Zhou & Ge, 2025), we control for the following

factors that could potentially influence corporate financialization tendencies. NESG, the sentiment score for non-ESG news, is a continuous and comparable value that ranges from 0 to 1, where a higher value indicates a more positive overall tone in firms' non-ESG-related news coverage in the year, while a lower value reflects a more negative tone. Size is the logarithm of companies' total assets. Capital denotes the ratio of capital expenditure to total assets. Fix is the company's fixed asset ratio. Lev represents the ratio of total liabilities to total assets. ROA is the ratio between a company's net profit and total assets. TQ is the Tobin's Q value. Dual is a dummy variable that determines whether the CEO holds dual roles that takes the value of 1 if the CEO also serves as the chair of the board of directors and 0 otherwise. ### 5.2. Robustness tests

#### 5.2.1. Instrumental variable regression

Our benchmark estimation results may be affected by potential endogeneity issues. ESG news sentiment can reduce a company's financialization by alleviating financing constraints and increasing real investment. Furthermore, companies engaged in higher financialization have stronger motivations and abilities to engage in media buying and exaggerated ESG investment publicity to manipulate positive media sentiment and conceal their financialization risks.

To address this, we use two instrumental variables (IVs), the number of newspaper types in the company's province (IV1) and regional per capita newspaper printing volume (IV2), calculated as the ratio of newspaper printing volume to the local population. IV1 and IV2 reflect local news media industry development, which influences the degree of media attention on local companies' ESG performance. Therefore, these IVs meet the relevance condition. Additionally, regional newspaper types and circulation do not directly impact a company's financialization, satisfying the exogeneity condition.

The IV regression results are presented in Table 3. The first-stage results in columns (1) and (3) reveal that the regression coefficients for IV1 and IV2 are significantly positive at the 1 % level. The Kleibergen–Paap (K–P) rk LM statistics, Cragg–Donald (C–D) Wald F values, and K–P Wald rk F values confirm no under-identification or weak IV issues, indicating the appropriateness of the selected IVs. In the second-stage regression results in columns (2) and (4), the regression coefficient for ESG remains negative at the 5 % level. This indicates that our benchmark results remain robust after addressing potential endogeneity issues from reverse causality.

#### 5.2.6. Placebo test

This study references Singh and Chanda (2021), using a placebo test to exclude the possible influence of unobservable factors on the estimated results. First, we randomly shuffle ESG news sentiment data for companies in different cities within the same year to generate random ESG news sentiment variables, allowing most companies to receive ESG sentiment scores of companies from other cities for that year. This method preserves the average trend of ESG news sentiment over time while severing the connection between regional media and corporate financial asset allocation. We then subject the randomly assigned ESG sentiment variables to regression testing on corporate financialization. Fig. 1 illustrates the distribution of ESG regression coefficients following 500 iterations. The results demonstrate that the baseline conclusions are robust.

#### 5.2.7. Omitted variable test

Although model (1) incorporates rigorous FEs to control for various multidimensional factors that may influence corporate financialization practices, the potential influence of unobserved firm-level or external factors cannot be entirely ruled out. To address this concern, we use firms' fixed asset growth rate (Fix) as a benchmark variable to estimate the potential strength of omitted variables, as Fix has the largest absolute coefficient among the control variables reported in column (4) of Table 2.

The four data points in Fig. 2 respectively represent scenarios in which no omitted variable is included, and omitted variables are assumed to exist at one to three times the strength of Fix. In Fig. 2a, the parentheses next to each point indicate the estimated coefficients of ESG, while the parentheses in Fig. 2b indicate the corresponding t-statistics. All four data points yield negative coefficient estimates, and all associated t-statistics are statistically significant and located to the left of the red threshold line. #### 5.3.2. Weakening the profit-driven investment motive

When companies that are motivated by positive ESG news sentiment undertake real investment projects to meet stakeholders' expectations of implementing ESG principles, they can also promote the long-term sustainable development of their core business (Bardos et al., 2020; Benlemlih & Bitar, 2018; Flammer, 2018; Friede et al., 2015). These investments also reflect companies' commitment to social and environmental responsibilities through digitalization and green initiatives.

To capture this, we reference Chen et al. (2022), measuring the proportion of total investments reported in the firm's annual report related to digital fixed assets and intangible assets as the digital investment indicator (digit). We calculate the green investment indicator (green) by summing the costs of environmental protection projects under construction disclosed in the firm's annual report and dividing it by total investment. The results in columns (1) and (2) of Table 6 reveal that the regression coefficients of ESG for digital and green investments are significantly positive. This confirms that positive ESG news sentiment significantly encourages companies to increase real investments in digital and green projects.

Furthermore, real investment projects' continuation relies heavily on external financing resources. High costs of financing real investments often lead companies to prefer lower-cost financial products such as stocks. Therefore, we measure corporate financing costs using two indicators, Cost1 and Cost2, defined as follows: Cost1 = (Interest Expenses + Fee and Commissions + Other Financial Expenses)/Total Liabilities; Cost2 = (Interest Expenses/Average Balance of Long-term and Short-term Liabilities at the end and beginning of the period). The regression results in columns (3) and (4) of Table 6 indicate that the coefficients for ESG are significantly negative. This indicates that positive ESG news sentiment conveys signals of stable operations and credible value investment objectives, reducing the risk perceptions in preloan assessments and postloan monitoring and lowering financing costs for companies. With better motivation and resources to engage in real investments, companies' profit-driven motives for financial asset allocation are significantly weakened.

Finally, we investigate performance changes resulting from the shift in the relationship between real and financial investments induced by ESG news sentiment, first introducing the difference in returns between financial and real investments (Delta) as an explanatory variable. The results in column (5) of Table 6 reveal that ESG news sentiment increases the performance gap between financial and real investments in the current period. A possible explanation is that while digital and green investments continue to expand, the benefits of long-term investments take time to materialize. This delay potentially causes a short-term decline in the return on real investments, widening the gap between financial and real investment returns. The results in column (6) of Table 6 further validate this perspective, demonstrating that ESG news sentiment significantly enhances the company's future core business performance (Msr) $ ^{12} $. This demonstrates that ESG news sentiment encourages companies to undertake long-term real investments that support sustainable development, improving future operational performance. Therefore, the inhibitory effect of ESG news sentiment on corporate financialization is more enduring.

#### 5.4.3. Regional heterogeneity

Digital finance is supported by digital technology and vast amounts of data and can collect, identify, and process enterprise information more effectively than traditional finance, allowing for precise financial risk identification and management (Chen et al., 2019). This capability increases the efficiency of regional credit resource allocation. This study uses each city's Digital Inclusive Finance Development Index to quantify regional digital finance development (Du et al., 2023), using the median of its sample as a grouping criterion. Columns (3) and (4) of Table 8 demonstrate that the impact coefficient of ESG on corporate financialization is significantly negative in areas with better digital finance development. In contrast, the coefficient is insignificant in areas with poorer development. The impact of ESG news sentiment depends on the dissemination and reception of information such as a company's reputation in the financial system. In regions with advanced digital finance development, the soft information conveyed by ESG news has a crucial influence on financial institutions' lending decisions. Optimistic ESG news sentiment can reduce the mismatch of credit resources between financial institutions and companies, suppressing corporate financialization.

With the advancement of ESG concepts, high-quality and sustainable urban development increasingly relies on effective urban ESG governance strategies (Teixeira Dias et al., 2023). Therefore, we further explore the relationship between cities, the news media, and businesses in promoting sustainable development. Using text analysis of government work reports, we identify 65 keywords, $ ^{14} $ including “low-carbon development,” “harmonious communities,” and “innovative governance,” and the frequency of these keywords serves as a proxy variable for urban ESG governance, with the sample median used for grouping tests. The results in columns (5) and (6) of Table 8 indicate that ESG significantly inhibits the financialization of companies in cities with high ESG governance intensity, and the effect is insignificant in cities with low ESG governance intensity. This finding indicates that companies consider the cost pressure associated with government ESG regulatory oversight when balancing asset allocation and may increase investment in physical projects that contribute to sustainable development, suppressing corporate financialization.

This is because ESG news sentiment primarily suppresses financialization by enhancing corporate external financing capabilities and reducing internal management's short-term investment motivations. Under the pressure of green credit-related policies, positive sentiment in environmental news directly affects a company's external financing ability, resulting in a stronger financial suppression effect. Furthermore, positive sentiment in governance-related news such as shareholders' rights protection can directly enhance shareholders' confidence in management. This encourages investment in corporate sustainable development and effectively suppresses financialization.

In the baseline model, the explanatory variable representing ESG news sentiment captures the overall sentiment expressed in all ESG-related news reports associated with a firm during a given year. However, this composite measure does not differentiate between specific types of sentiment, which makes it difficult to determine which particular emotional tone—positive, neutral, or negative—is more effective in constraining corporate financialization. To address this limitation, referencing Yu et al. (2023), we classify ESG news into positive, neutral, and negative categories to enable a more nuanced investigation into the varying effects of different sentiment types on corporate financialization. Specifically, we measure the positive (ESG_pos), neutral (ESG_neu), and negative (ESG_neg) news ratios by the proportion of positive, neutral, and negative reports in a company's total ESG news coverage. Columns (4)–(6) in Table 9 indicate that while all increased ESG news sentiment suppresses corporate financialization, neutral ESG news has the most significant effect. This is because neutral ESG news avoids excessive media sentiment intervention in the market (Boudoukh et al., 2019; Snyder Jr & Strömberg, 2010) and excessive pressure on management (He & Tian, 2013). Moreover, commercial banks tend to make rational credit choices based on economic efficiency (Firth et al., 2009). Therefore, neutral ESG news, which is not subject to emotional manipulation, reveals more information and is more likely to be considered by financial institutions when making lending decisions.

## 6. Conclusions and policy suggestions

Positing that ESG principles provide a framework for guiding corporate financial investments, this study constructs an innovative ESG news sentiment index based on more than 2 million ESG news articles about publicly listed companies from 2007 to 2022 to examine its relationship with corporate financialization. The results indicate that positive ESG news sentiment significantly inhibits corporate financialization. This governance function is achieved through increased ESG financial support for enterprises, which weakens the risk-averse motives of financialization, and rising internal motivation and external resources for real investments, which diminish the profit-driven motives of financialization. Finally, we determine that ESG news sentiment's governance effect on financialization is more pronounced for nonrecessionary firms, high-tech enterprises, heavily polluting companies, in regions with better digital finance development, and in areas with stronger ESG governance intensity. Additionally, when focused on environmental and governance dimensions and presented neutrally, ESG news sentiment has a stronger inhibitory effect on corporate financialization.

Our research has several practical implications. First, it is essential to strengthen the social responsibility of the media and recognize the governance role of news media in corporate affairs. In emerging market economies, where legal and administrative mechanisms may be inadequate for curbing opportunistic practices such as corporate financialization, the media can have an effective regulatory role at a relatively low cost. By increasing the coverage of corporate ESG-related news, the media provides real, timely, and rich information about corporate ESG performance. This enables companies that fulfill ESG responsibilities to gain broader market recognition, guiding more firms toward ethical and compliant business practices. Second, companies should actively fulfill their ESG

responsibilities and use ESG principles to guide their business and investment decisions. By reducing short-term speculative practices and focusing on the long-term core business development, companies can gain the trust and support of a broader range of stakeholders. Third, banks should enhance the application of digital technology in their lending processes. By improving their ability to identify and analyze soft information before loans are granted and to monitor the flow of funds afterward, banks can better identify high-quality companies that prioritize social responsibility and long-term development, improving credit resource allocation efficiency.

This study has some limitations. Our research primarily relies on ESG news reports collected from the Baidu News website. Although it does not include all relevant news channels, it offers a certain level of representation. With the development of other information dissemination media (e.g., social media), future research can further explore the impact of multisource media information on corporate financialization and other strategic decisions.
