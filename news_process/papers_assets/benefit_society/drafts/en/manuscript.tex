\begin{abstract}
\NoteThis{Final abstract pending.}
How is the digital transformation of banks evaluated by the media, and
how does that evaluation relate to bank performance? We transpose the
language-embedding bipolar anchor projection of Jha--Liu--Manela (2026,
\emph{RFS}) from the country-year macro unit to the bank-year firm
level: for each bank--FinTech news article's embedding vector $s_j$, we
take the projection $a_j=\cos(s_j,\,\mathrm{pos}-\mathrm{neg})$ on the
semantic axis spanned by 10 fintech-specific positive/negative anchor
pairs and aggregate to a bank-year ``operating-benefit attitude''
(\code{benefit\_mean\_full}). The measure is computed under two
co-primary embedding models from independent vendors (Alibaba
Qwen3-Embedding-8B, 4{,}096-d; OpenAI text-embedding-3-large,
3{,}072-d), reported side by side as mutual robustness. On a bank-year
panel built from Chinese bank--FinTech media coverage (production
target corpus of 18{,}198{,}978 articles, 2007--2025), we test the
effect of operating-benefit attitude on bank profitability (ROA) under
bank and year fixed effects (bank-clustered plus wild-cluster-bootstrap
inference; leave-one-out peer instruments), and examine its asymmetric
transmission to token versus substantive FinTech adoption. We find
\NoteThis{key result: baseline coefficients, significance, and
mechanism results to be back-filled from the production panel}\@.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Over the past decade and a half, Chinese banking has undergone a deep
digital transformation: from mobile payments and online credit to
AI-based risk control, FinTech has become the core vocabulary of bank
strategic narratives. At the same time, the media are among the most
important information intermediaries in a bank's information
environment: depositors, regulators, and investors form their
perception of a bank's digital transformation largely through news
coverage \parencite{tetlock2007content,fang2009media}. The raw-corpus
census underlying this paper spans 18{,}198{,}978 media reports
(2007--2025, 3{,}740 media outlets, with newspaper-type reports
accounting for 92.93\% of all rows), documenting sustained, dense media
attention to banks. Yet the existing literature focuses mostly on the
\emph{quantity} of coverage (exposure) or the \emph{direction} of
sentiment (positive/negative tone); it lacks a systematic firm-level
measure of the \emph{attitude intensity} with which the media assess
whether a bank's FinTech operations benefit its operating performance.

The question we pose and answer is: is the media's attitude toward a
bank's FinTech operating benefit associated with bank performance, and
if so, how? We transpose the language-embedding bipolar anchor
projection of \textcite{jha2026benefit} from the country-year macro
unit to the bank-year firm level: for each bank--FinTech article's
embedding vector $s_j$, we take the projection
$a_j=\cos(s_j,\,\mathrm{pos}-\mathrm{neg})$ on the semantic axis
spanned by 10 fintech-specific frozen anchor pairs and aggregate to the
bank-year operating-benefit attitude \code{benefit\_mean\_full}.

For identification, the baseline regression uses a within estimator
with bank and year fixed effects (absorb(bank\_id year)) and controls
for coverage proxies (\code{ln\_news\_coverage}, \code{media\_breadth},
\code{n\_articles}) to separate ``how much a bank is covered'' (the
extensive margin) from ``how it is evaluated'' (the intensive margin)
\parencite{fang2009media}; inference is based on bank-level clustering
complemented by the wild cluster bootstrap
\parencite{roodman2019boottest}. To address the endogeneity of media
attitude, we estimate 2SLS with the leave-one-out mean attitude of
same-type peer banks (\code{leave\_one\_out\_peer\_benefit\_iv}) as the
primary instrument and the cross-province same-economic-zone
leave-one-out mean (\code{zone\_leave1out\_benefit\_iv}) as the
fallback, disclosing exclusion-restriction limitations following
\textcite{manski1993reflection} and \textcite{leary2014peer}. As for
results, \NoteThis{results preview: baseline, mechanism, and
heterogeneity conclusions to be back-filled after production-panel
($T=19$, 110 banks) regressions}\@.

This paper makes four contributions. First, a measurement
contribution: we transpose bipolar anchor projection from the
country-year macro unit to the bank-year firm level, constructing 10
fintech-specific frozen anchor pairs (versus J-L-M's 5 broad-finance
pairs), with the measure computed in parallel under embedding models
from two independent vendors, reported side by side as mutual
robustness. Second, a construct-differentiation contribution: relative
to the text-measurement literature
\parencite{gentzkow2019text,li2021culture,hoberg2016text}, we measure
\emph{attitude projection intensity} rather than sentiment direction,
exposure counts, or binary substantiveness labels, explicitly
distinguishing this paper from the tone, attention, substance, locus,
and network papers in the same research portfolio. Third, a mechanism
contribution: we bring symbolic--substantive decoupling theory
\parencite{westphal1994substance,fiss2006symbolic} into the
bank-FinTech setting and test the asymmetric transmission of media
attitude to token versus substantive adoption. Fourth, a
reproducibility contribution: the entire measurement chain (LLM gating,
embeddings, anchors) is reproducible under frozen parameters
(temperature=0, seed=42, byte-frozen prompt v8) and caching.

The paper proceeds as follows. Section~\ref{sec:lit} reviews the
literature; Section~\ref{sec:theory} develops hypotheses;
Section~\ref{sec:method} presents methods and identification;
Section~\ref{sec:data} describes the data;
Sections~\ref{sec:main}--\ref{sec:robust} report baseline, mechanism,
heterogeneity, and robustness results; Section~\ref{sec:discussion}
discusses limitations and contributions, and Section~\ref{sec:concl}
concludes.

\section{Literature Review}\label{sec:lit}
This paper speaks to four strands of literature: (1)~embedding
projection and text-based measurement (the measurement foundation being
Jha--Liu--Manela 2026 \emph{RFS}); (2)~media coverage, information
intermediation, and bank outcomes; (3)~symbolic--substantive decoupling
and narrative--performance divergence; and (4)~FinTech and bank
performance, together with identification and inference.

\subsection{Embedding Projection and Text-Based Measurement}\label{sec:lit:embed}
Text entered economics as data with the foundational survey of
\textcite{gentzkow2019text}, whose ``text $\to$ numerical
representation $\to$ regression input'' framework is the methodological
blueprint for our measurement chain; \textcite{ash2023text} further
systematize the case for representing text with embedding vectors and
performing economic measurement with vector geometry. On concrete
constructions, \textcite{gennaro2022emotion} build a continuous measure
from the relative distance of document embeddings to two reference
semantic poles---isomorphic to our projection
$a_j=\cos(s_j,\,\mathrm{pos}-\mathrm{neg})$;
\textcite{li2021culture} construct an embedding-based corporate-culture
measure with a battery of validity checks, the benchmark for
``embedding measure plus validation''; and \textcite{hoberg2016text}
rebuild industry classification from text cosine similarity, founding
the family of similarity-based economic measures. Our measurement base
directly extends \textcite{jha2026benefit}. On the engine side, the
technical details of the canonical-slot model are given by
\textcite{zhang2025qwen3}, its financial-domain (including Chinese)
validity anchored by benchmark evaluation \parencite{tang2025finmteb};
\textcite{gabaix2025asset} provide frontier legitimacy for using
embedding vectors directly as financial econometric variables.

\subsection{Media Coverage, Information Intermediation, and Bank Outcomes}\label{sec:lit:media}
\textcite{tetlock2007content} established the paradigm that media tone
affects economic outcomes; \textcite{fang2009media} showed that
coverage itself is a pre-determined visibility confounder that must be
controlled when estimating attitude effects---the direct precedent for
our coverage-proxy control set. In the Chinese setting,
\textcite{you2018pen} characterize the tone differences between
state-controlled and market-oriented media and their governance
consequences; \textcite{gurun2012hype} document the link between local
media slant and firm value, also supporting the geographic granularity
of our economic-zone instrument. Closest to us,
\textcite{fang2024mediasentiment} show that bank-level media sentiment
in China affects deposit stability and systemic risk. Together, this
strand establishes the premise that bank-year media attitude has real
operating consequences.

\subsection{Symbolic--Substantive Decoupling and Narrative--Performance Divergence}\label{sec:lit:decouple}
\textcite{westphal1994substance} founded the decoupling theory that
adoption need not equal implementation; \textcite{westphal2023systemic}
extends symbolic-management theory to the CSR and corporate-purpose
era. \textcite{fiss2006symbolic} formalize symbolic strategic
management via framing plus decoupling; \textcite{kim2015greenwash}
formalize disclosure--performance deviation as a measurable construct;
and \textcite{duchin2025greenwashing} provide direct asset-market
evidence of divergence between talk and real behavior. This strand
anchors the theory and banking context for the token-versus-substantive
asymmetric transmission in H2/H3, and connects to the
evidence-to-impact chain of \parencite{distel2026evidence}.

\subsection{FinTech and Bank Performance: China Context, Identification, and Inference}\label{sec:lit:fintech}
The survey of \textcite{berg2022fintech} maps the fintech--bank
research landscape; \textcite{cheng2020fintech} provide direct Chinese
evidence that bank FinTech reduces credit risk; and
\textcite{wu2025cooperation} offer panel evidence on Chinese
bank--fintech strategic cooperation and risk-taking, jointly motivating
why we measure fintech operating-benefit attitude in Chinese banking.
On identification, peer-mean instruments face the reflection problem
\parencite{manski1993reflection}, and \textcite{leary2014peer} is the
classic implementation of leave-one-out peer instruments; on inference,
the stepwise FWER correction of \textcite{romano2005stepwise} and the
wild cluster bootstrap of \textcite{roodman2019boottest} underpin our
inference declaration.

Taken together, the gap left by the literature is the absence of a
firm-level, fintech-operating-benefit-oriented media-attitude measure
built on embedding projection intensity, along with systematic evidence
on its relation to bank performance and adoption behavior. This paper
fills that gap.

\section{Theory and Hypotheses}\label{sec:theory}

This section develops hypotheses from two theoretical threads:
information intermediation and symbolic--substantive decoupling. All
statements below are \emph{theoretical predictions}, not obtained
results; H1 is the only confirmatory hypothesis, locked by PAP
v1.0-final (spec \code{inference.confirmatory\_family}), while H2--H4
are exploratory and subject to Romano--Wolf family correction (see
Section~\ref{sec:method:infer}).

\subsection{H1: Operating-Benefit Attitude and Bank Performance (confirmatory)}
Under the information-intermediation channel, positive media attitude
toward a bank's FinTech operating benefit improves the bank's
information environment and reputational capital: depositors are more
willing to maintain deposit relationships, counterparties demand lower
risk premia, and customers adopt digital channels more readily
\parencite{tetlock2007content,fang2024mediasentiment}. Crucially, media
operating-benefit attitude is the \emph{intensive margin} of the
information environment (how a bank is evaluated), distinct from the
\emph{extensive margin} of coverage (how much it is covered)
\parencite{fang2009media}; conditional on coverage proxies, independent
variation in attitude should transmit to profitability through
reputation and information channels. Hence:

\textbf{H1 (confirmatory)}: bank-year operating-benefit attitude
(\code{benefit\_mean\_full}) is positively associated with bank
profitability (\code{roa}); the predicted sign is positive.

\subsection{H2: Attitude and Token Adoption (exploratory)}
Symbolic-management theory holds that organizations respond to external
legitimacy pressure with low-cost symbolic gestures
\parencite{westphal1994substance,fiss2006symbolic}. A favorable media
attitude environment lowers the marginal cost of symbolic
pronouncements and amplifies their expected legitimacy payoff, raising
the propensity for token FinTech adoption.

\textbf{H2 (exploratory, PAP secondary)}: \code{benefit\_mean\_full} is
positively associated with \code{token\_adopt\_kw\_p50}; the predicted
sign is positive.

\subsection{H3: Symbolic--Substantive Asymmetric Transmission (exploratory)}
Substantive adoption requires real resource commitment and
organizational change, so its sensitivity to the external opinion
environment should be lower than that of symbolic gestures; the
decoupling literature predicts that talk responds faster and more
strongly than action \parencite{kim2015greenwash,duchin2025greenwashing}.

\textbf{H3 (exploratory)}: the transmission of
\code{benefit\_mean\_full} to \code{subst\_adopt\_kw\_p50} and
\code{hidden\_adopt\_kw\_p50} is weaker than to
\code{token\_adopt\_kw\_p50} (theoretical prediction; coefficient
comparison awaits production results).

\subsection{H4: Social-Function Counterparts (exploratory extension, not pre-registered)}
If the ``benefit-society/operating-benefit'' narrative reflects real
operating orientation, it should leave traces in social-function
channels such as credit allocation and liquidity creation.

\textbf{H4 (exploratory extension, outside the PAP and explicitly not
pre-registered)}: \code{benefit\_mean\_full} is positively associated
with \code{inclusive\_loan\_growth} and \code{liquidity\_creation};
this test depends on the arrival of the financial panel (theoretical
prediction).

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Sample and Data}
The bank--year panel covers approximately 164 bank--years
(2017--2018). This count is a \emph{development-period snapshot}
($T=2$ dev) used only for pipeline and specification validation; the
production target panel aggregates the full census scope of
18{,}198{,}978 media reports (2007--2025, $T=19$) into a bank-year
panel covering 110
banks, and the PAP sample target (n\_bank\_min $= 200$, $T=19$, obs
$\geq 3000$) is yet another stage-specific accounting---the three
must not be mixed.
\NoteThis{TODO: all sample-size figures in text and tables to be
back-filled uniformly once the production panel is finalized; no
interim sample counts are to be computed by hand.}
All bank--year observations are processed by the
\code{news\_process} pipeline (Phase 9b baseline; see
\code{papers\_assets/V6\_METHODOLOGY\_CHANGES.md}).

\subsection{Tone Annotation (F1: Three-Family LLM Validation)}
We employ three independent LLM families for sentiment annotation:
\code{gpt-5.4-nano} (OpenAI), \code{gemini-3.1-flash-lite}
(Google), and \code{deepseek-v4-flash} (DeepSeek). The OpenAI and
Google models are routed through yunwu.ai (\code{/v1/chat/completions},
OpenAI-compatible); DeepSeek is called directly at
\code{api.deepseek.com}. All calls use \code{temperature=0} and
\code{seed=42} with a byte-frozen prompt (version v8). Inter-family agreement reported as
Cohen's $\kappa$ and Krippendorff's $\alpha$; majority vote produces the
final label, with ties or no-vote outcomes triggering up to 80 rounds of
deterministic tie-breaking retries and, if still unresolved, conservatively
coded as \code{neutral} (rather than left as undefined).

\subsection{Article-Level Coding (F2: Decontamination)}
Articles where all three families fail are coded \emph{missing}, not
\code{neutral}. Sentiment ties or no-vote outcomes are not treated as
missing: they trigger up to 80 rounds of deterministic tie-breaking
retries and, if still unresolved, are conservatively coded as neutral.
Bank--FinTech relevance failing majority verification is also missing.
This eliminates the 81.7\,\%
contamination in pre-v6 ``neutral'' labels.

Article retention is gated on the full-text track only
(\code{llm\_verified\_full}); the entity-context (\code{ctx}) and
same-sentence co-occurrence (\code{ss}) tracks are conditional-on-full
robustness tracks whose row universe is a subset of the full-track
retained set. Relative to earlier any-track-verified vintages, this
stricter gate mechanically shrinks \code{n\_articles} and derived
shares (\code{fintech\_share}, \code{attention\_pct})---a
definitional, conservative level shift, not a data error.

\subsection{Zero-Observation Semantics (F3: NaN Main Path)}
For bank--years with \code{n\_articles=0}, similarity-based features
(\code{auth\_sim\_*}, \code{benefit\_*}, \code{article\_sim\_*}),
ratios (\code{token\_adopt\_*}, \code{hidden\_adopt\_*},
\code{subst\_adopt\_*}), and \code{fintech\_intensity\_mean} are coded
as \code{NaN}. Counts remain at 0. Listwise deletion follows Allison
(2001) / Heckman (1979).

\subsection{Bartik IV Pruning (F4)}
Of approximately 30 candidate Bartik instruments, 28 are removed in v6
for violating the exogeneity criteria of Goldsmith-Pinkham et
al.\ \parencite{goldsmith2020bartik}. Retained: \code{bartik\_type\_iv\_full}
and \code{prov\_leave1out\_tone\_full}.

\subsection{Embedding Backbone (Co-Primary Dual Track; F5)}
The bipolar anchor projection underlying
\code{benefit\_mean\_full}---and every other embedding-derived
feature---is computed under two co-primary embedding models from
independent vendors, reported side by side as mutual robustness:
Alibaba \code{Qwen3-Embedding-8B} (4{,}096-d, accessed via the
SiliconFlow API; canonical unsuffixed columns) and OpenAI
\code{text-embedding-3-large} (3{,}072-d; \code{\_lg}-suffixed
columns), under strict column parity (each feature exists in both
tracks or in neither). Each track enters its own just-identified
specification; the two are never pooled into one over-identified
system. Earlier pipeline vintages used a Google Gemini embedding in
the canonical slot; as of pipeline v3.15.0 (June 2026) the canonical
slot is Alibaba \code{Qwen3-Embedding-8B}, with all embedding-derived
features regenerated under a controlled full re-embedding and
embedding-space semantic validity gated by a Spearman
rank-correlation check ($\rho \geq 0.80$). The sentiment-annotation
LLM ensemble (F1) is unaffected by this change: embedding models and
sentiment LLMs are disjoint pipeline components.

The dual-track design is not decorative: in the Qwen embedding space
the negative pole of one of the ten frozen anchor pairs (anchor
pair~3, the credit-quality dimension) projects with an inverted sign
relative to the \code{\_lg}
space, while the axis-sharpness diagnostic still passes. Rather than
re-tuning the frozen anchors post hoc, we keep both co-primary tracks
exactly as specified and read the affected measure jointly across the
two vendor spaces---precisely the failure mode the cross-vendor
dual-track architecture is designed to absorb, and we disclose it
here.

\subsection{Identification Strategy and Instruments (Just-Identified per Track)}\label{sec:method:iv}
Media attitude is not randomly assigned: better-performing banks may
both attract more favorable coverage and actively manage media
relations. Beyond the two-way fixed effects and coverage-proxy
controls, we address remaining endogeneity with instrumental
variables. The primary instrument is the leave-one-out mean
operating-benefit attitude of same-type peer banks
(\code{leave\_one\_out\_peer\_benefit\_iv}): peer media-attitude shocks
transmit to the focal bank's media attitude through the industry-wide
information environment but---under the exclusion restriction---do not
enter the focal bank's own ROA through other channels. The fallback
instrument is the cross-province same-economic-zone leave-one-out mean
(\code{zone\_leave1out\_benefit\_iv}), identifying off regional common
shocks; \code{\_lag1} lagged variants serve as additional robustness.
Peer-mean instruments face the reflection problem, and the exclusion
restriction rests on untestable assumptions; we disclose this
limitation candidly following \textcite{manski1993reflection} and
\textcite{leary2014peer}.

Each text track receives a mirrored own-track instrument set: the
entity-context endogenous twin \code{benefit\_mean\_ctx} is paired with
\code{lpeer\_benefit\_iv\_ctx} and
\code{zone\_leave1out\_benefit\_iv\_ctx}; the same-sentence twin
\code{benefit\_mean\_ss} with \code{lpeer\_benefit\_iv\_ss} and
\code{zone\_leave1out\_benefit\_iv\_ss}. Each track forms its own
just-identified 2SLS, reported side by side (template
25\_tableA12\_iv\_2sls); pooling the full/ctx/ss instruments into a
single over-identified Hansen $J$ system is \emph{strictly
prohibited}---the three tracks are co-equal mutual-robustness measures,
not multiple instruments for one construct.

\subsection{Inference Declaration}\label{sec:method:infer}
Primary inference uses bank-clustered (cluster $=$ bank\_id) standard
errors as the baseline; given the possibly small number of bank
clusters, the wild cluster bootstrap serves as the headline inference
\parencite{roodman2019boottest,mackinnon2017wild,cameron2008bootstrap},
with bank $\times$ year two-way clustering as a comparison
\parencite{cameron2015practitioner}. The confirmatory family contains
a single primary hypothesis (\code{benefit\_mean\_full} $\to$
\code{roa}, main specification M4); a single hypothesis requires no
FWER correction. All exploratory hypothesis families (ctx/ss tracks,
pos/neg decomposition, secondary outcomes, \code{is\_listed} and
subsample heterogeneity) receive the Romano--Wolf stepdown correction
on their p-value families \parencite{romano2005stepwise}. A
distinction is essential: the robustness ladders themselves (dual
embeddings, three text tracks, six dimension tracks, criteria ladder)
are co-equal mutual-robustness measures, not multiple-testing families,
and no FWER is applied across ladders. That the same ctx/ss estimates
also enter the exploratory Romano--Wolf family above is not a
contradiction: the correction operates within that hypothesis family
only and is never pooled across ladders.

\section{Main Results}\label{sec:main}
The baseline specification is a two-way fixed-effects within regression
on the bank-year panel:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:baseline}
\mathit{ROA}_{it} \;=\; \beta\,\mathit{Benefit}_{it} \;+\; \Gamma' X_{it}
\;+\; \alpha_i \;+\; \delta_t \;+\; \varepsilon_{it},
\end{equation}
where $\mathit{Benefit}_{it}$ is \code{benefit\_mean\_full},
$\alpha_i$ and $\delta_t$ are bank and year fixed effects (implemented
in Stata as \code{reghdfe} + absorb(bank\_id year)), $X_{it}$ is the
coverage-proxy control set (Section~\ref{sec:data}), and standard
errors are clustered at the bank level and complemented by the wild
cluster bootstrap (Section~\ref{sec:method:infer}). Table 2 adds fixed
effects and controls progressively from M1 to M4; M4 (two-way fixed
effects plus full controls) is the PAP-locked confirmatory main
specification; under H1 we expect $\hat\beta > 0$. The table will show
the within association between operating-benefit attitude and ROA; the
coefficient captures the co-movement of within-bank, across-year
variation in attitude and profitability, net of time-invariant bank
heterogeneity and common year shocks.

\section{Mechanism}\label{sec:mech}
The mechanism tests examine the transmission of operating-benefit
attitude into FinTech adoption behavior, corresponding to H2/H3. The
dependent variables are three adoption-share measures (PAP secondary):
token \code{token\_adopt\_kw\_p50}, hidden
\code{hidden\_adopt\_kw\_p50}, and substantive
\code{subst\_adopt\_kw\_p50}. As fractional response variables they are
estimated under a dual-model design: fractional logit
\parencite{papke1996fractional} and an OLS linear probability model
(with bank fixed effects; spec B2 dual-model convention), coefficients
read side by side in marginal-effect terms. The interpretive frame is
symbolic--substantive decoupling
\parencite{westphal1994substance,fiss2006symbolic}: under H3, the token
channel coefficient should exceed the subst/hidden channels. All of
the above are exploratory; the p-value family receives the Romano--Wolf
correction.

cross-channel comparisons to be back-filled from production regressions

\section{Heterogeneity}\label{sec:het}
The main heterogeneity dimension is listing status
(\code{is\_listed}): listed banks face stronger capital-market

\section{Robustness}\label{sec:robust}
All robustness ladders in this section share one statistical

robustness}, each entering its own just-identified specification and
reported side by side; the measures of any ladder are never pooled into
a single over-identified Hansen $J$ system, and ladders are orthogonal
and never crossed.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Limitations of Measurement and Identification}\label{sec:disc:limit}
\NoteThis{Following Jha-Liu-Manela (2026) Section~6.1 narrative convention,
we acknowledge five limitations:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Panel temporal span}: the current 84-bank, 2-year panel
        ($T=2$, 2017--2018) limits time-series tests; event-study around
        fintech regulatory shocks and local-projection impulse responses are
        deferred until panel expansion to $T\geq10$ (M0.5 milestone).
  \item \textbf{Article-level vs sentence-level projection}: where J-L-M
        project sentence embeddings (Google Books Ngram corpus is
        sentence-level), our news corpus is article-level. The bipolar
        projection method is unchanged; the unit of measurement differs.
        \code{core/text/cn\_text\_utils.py} (v3.5.2) provides sentence
        splitter infrastructure for future sentence-level robustness.
  \item \textbf{Embedding model choice}: measures are computed under
        two co-primary API embedding models from independent vendors
        (Alibaba \texttt{Qwen3-Embedding-8B}, 4{,}096-d;
        OpenAI \texttt{text-embedding-3-large}, 3{,}072-d) reported
        side by side as mutual robustness (earlier vintages variously
        used a local \texttt{text2vec} model and a Google Gemini
        embedding; both retired). Sensitivity beyond these two vendors
        is acknowledged but not exhausted in the current version.
  \item \textbf{Cohort scope}: analysis restricted to the PAP v1.0-final
        84-bank cohort (Big-5 + joint-stock + city-commercial); full
        1839-bank universe is not analyzed. Generalization to non-cohort
        institutions requires separate empirical test.
  \item \textbf{Cross-corpus comparison}: J-L-M analyze Google Books Ngram
        (multi-language, 1870--2009); we analyze Chinese financial news
        (single-language, 2017--2018). Corpus genre, publication lag, and
        editorial filtering differ. Bank fixed effects absorb stable
        cross-bank measurement error.
\end{enumerate}
}

\subsection{Contribution Statement}\label{sec:disc:contrib}
\NoteThis{Our contribution beyond Jha-Liu-Manela (2026) is three-fold,
distinguishing this paper from a China-replication of J-L-M:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Unit of observation}: bank-year firm-level (vs country-year
        macro), enabling within-bank within-year identification with
        finer-grained fixed effects.
  \item \textbf{Outcome variables}: Token/Hidden adoption mechanism
        (Distel-Grimpe-Poetz 2026, Westphal-Zajac 2001 decoupling framework),
        rather than J-L-M's macroeconomic GDP/credit/Gini outcomes.
  \item \textbf{Measurement design}: 10-pair fintech-specific anchor set
        with dual-track full/ctx projection (entity-context masking), versus
        J-L-M's 5-pair broad-finance canonical anchors. Section~\ref{sec:robust:a2}
        provides cross-validation between the two anchor families.
\end{enumerate}
}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:concl}
This paper transposes language-embedding bipolar anchor projection from
the country-year macro unit to the bank-year firm level, constructs the
operating-benefit attitude measure \code{benefit\_mean\_full} from
Chinese bank--FinTech media coverage, and tests its effects on bank
performance and FinTech adoption within a mutual-robustness
architecture of dual embeddings, three text tracks, per-track
just-identified instruments, and an inference ladder.
\NoteThis{Core empirical conclusion paragraph: direction, magnitude,
and the mechanism/heterogeneity picture to be back-filled after
production-panel (18{,}198{,}978 articles, 2007--2025, 110 banks)
regressions.}

Theoretically, the paper connects the information-intermediation
channel with symbolic--substantive decoupling theory: media
operating-benefit attitude, as the intensive margin of a bank's
information environment, may both reflect and shape the bank's digital
transformation path;
\NoteThis{the theoretical-implication sentence on token-vs-substantive
transmission asymmetry to be back-filled from results.}
For policy, the measure offers regulators a low-cost, reproducible
monitoring indicator of public discourse on bank digital
transformation: if \NoteThis{conditional policy-implication clause
(conditional on production results) to be back-filled}, then regulatory
communication on inclusive finance and bank digitalization could add
media operating-benefit attitude to its monitoring dashboard.

Several limitations remain. First, the panel is annual, so quarterly
or event-level dynamics cannot be captured; high-frequency event
studies (e.g., \code{car\_3day}) await the daily-data listed-bank
extension. Second, media coverage is itself endogenous to bank
behavior and editorial selection, and the instruments' exclusion
restriction rests on untestable assumptions
\parencite{manski1993reflection,leary2014peer}; conclusions should be
read as within associations combined with instrumental evidence, not
unconditional causality. Third, the sample covers the single Chinese
market, whose media institutional environment (state-media structure,
regulatory communication) is distinctive \parencite{you2018pen};
extrapolation to other banking systems warrants caution. Fourth, the
measure depends on embedding-model choice: the dual-vendor co-primary
architecture absorbs single-model failure modes (including the
disclosed sign flip of anchor pair 3), but sensitivity beyond the two
vendors is not exhausted.
