\title{Network Brokerage and Bank Performance:\\Causal Identification via Second-Order Peer Tone}
\author{\NoteThis{Author names}}
\date{\today}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We study how peer media tone propagates through the bank--bank
co-occurrence network endogenously generated by Chinese FinTech news
coverage, and the structural role of a bank's \emph{brokerage}
position in this contagion. Using a bank--year panel spanning
2007--2024 (a production target corpus of roughly 17 million articles
covering 110 banks), we define first-order peer tone
(\code{peer\_tone\_lag1\_full}) from a row-normalized co-occurrence
matrix $W$ and, exploiting the exclusion restriction of intransitive
triads \parencite{bramoulle2009identification}, instrument it with
second-order peer tone ($W^{2}\cdot\text{tone}$---the tone of
``peers of peers'') in a network 2SLS that circumvents the Manski
reflection problem \parencite{manski1993reflection}. We find
\NoteThis{key result: 2SLS coefficient of first-order peer-tone
spillover on ROA, significance, and economic magnitude pending
production-panel regressions}. Evidence on brokerage position
(Freeman betweenness, Bonacich eigenvector, Burt structural-hole
constraint) as explanatory, moderating, and heterogeneity carriers
indicates \NoteThis{network-position heterogeneity placeholder}.
The paper is the first to combine media co-occurrence brokerage
positions with second-order spillover instruments to identify
interbank sentiment contagion in China, and offers a measurement
framework for monitoring ``contagion-amplifier'' banks from a
macroprudential perspective.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Over the past decade and a half, Chinese banking has undergone a deep
digital transformation: FinTech has reshaped business boundaries from
payments and credit to wealth management, and the media has served as
one of the most important information intermediaries of this
transformation. Banks' FinTech strategies, risk events, and
regulatory developments enter the information sets of depositors,
investors, and regulators through massive news flows; media sentiment
has been shown to affect deposit stability and amplify bank systemic
risk \parencite{fang2024media}, and media amplification can even
catalyze bank runs \parencite{cookson2026social}. Yet the existing
literature focuses almost exclusively on a \emph{single bank's own}
media exposure and tone, overlooking a basic fact: banks never appear
in the news in isolation. When one FinTech article mentions several
banks together, it builds an information conduit among them; tens of
millions of such articles aggregate into an endogenous bank--bank
co-occurrence network. Can negative peer sentiment travel along this
network into a focal bank's operating performance? Are banks
occupying network \emph{brokerage} positions---bridging communities
and intermediating information---amplified victims of shocks, or
beneficiaries of an information advantage? These are the questions
this paper answers.

Identifying peer sentiment spillovers confronts the classic
reflection problem \parencite{manski1993reflection}: peer tone and
own tone are jointly driven by common shocks, so naively placing the
peer average on the right-hand side confounds endogenous peer
effects, exogenous contextual effects, and correlated effects. Our
identification strategy builds on the network econometrics of
\textcite{bramoulle2009identification}: as long as the co-occurrence
network contains intransitive triads (``a friend of my friend is not
my friend''), second-order peer tone $W^{2}\cdot\text{tone}$ can
affect the focal bank only through its direct peers and therefore
constitutes a valid instrument for the first-order spillover
$W\cdot\text{tone}$. We estimate a network 2SLS with the lagged
first-order peer tone (\code{peer\_tone\_lag1\_full}) as the
endogenous treatment, the second-order peer tone
(\code{peer\_tone\_2nd\_lag1\_full}) as the instrument, and ROA as
the outcome, with bank and year fixed effects throughout and standard
errors clustered at the province level supplemented by wild cluster
bootstrap.

This paper's division of labor with the adjacent papers in our
portfolio is explicit. Unlike P1 (firm-level tone), we identify
\emph{network-transmitted peer spillovers} rather than the
performance effect of a bank's own tone; unlike llm\_peer, our weight
matrix $W$ is built from \emph{observable co-occurrence events} in
news coverage rather than LLM-judged semantic peer relations; unlike
strategic\_diff, our explanatory variables are network positions and
spillover quantities rather than multi-text representation
distances. Centrality itself is not the spillover engine but a
threefold carrier---explanatory, moderating, and
heterogeneity-defining---a distinction that disciplines every
econometric specification in the paper.

We make four contributions. First, to our knowledge this is the
first paper to construct a bank--bank media co-occurrence network on
a corpus of tens of millions of Chinese bank news articles and to
identify the causal effect of peer sentiment contagion on bank
performance using second-order spillover instruments, carrying the
network peer-effects identification lineage
\parencite{bramoulle2009identification,bramoulle2020peer,boucher2024general}
into the media--banking setting. Second, we elevate brokerage
position---Freeman betweenness \parencite{freeman1978centrality},
Bonacich eigenvector centrality \parencite{bonacich1972factoring},
and Burt's structural-hole constraint
\parencite{burt1992structural}---from descriptive statistics into
testable carriers of the contagion mechanism, echoing the
institutional brokerage-network construct of
\textcite{han2024brokerage}. Third, methodologically, we demonstrate
a preregistration discipline: a single confirmatory headline
specification, a 22-centrality battery demoted wholesale---except
for its three preregistered members---to an exploratory appendix
under Romano--Wolf familywise error control
\parencite{romano2005stepwise}, and a reporting rule under which
multi-measure ladders are ``mutual robustness, no primacy'' with
each rung entering its own just-identified specification. Fourth,
for policy, we provide macroprudential authorities with a low-cost
monitoring dimension for sentiment contagion: identifying banks
occupying amplifier positions in the media network.

The remainder of the paper proceeds as follows. Section
\ref{sec:lit} reviews the literature; Section \ref{sec:theory}
develops hypotheses; Section \ref{sec:method} presents the data
pipeline, network construction, and identification design; Section
\ref{sec:data} describes variables and descriptive statistics;
Section \ref{sec:main} reports baseline results; Sections
\ref{sec:mech} and \ref{sec:het} examine mechanisms and
heterogeneity; Section \ref{sec:robust} reports robustness; Section
\ref{sec:concl} concludes.

\section{Literature Review}\label{sec:lit}

\subsection{Peer-Effects Identification and Network Econometrics}

Since \textcite{manski1993reflection} posed the reflection problem,
the central difficulty in peer-effects identification has been
separating endogenous peer effects from exogenous contextual effects
and correlated effects. \textcite{bramoulle2009identification} show
that when the social network contains intransitive triads, the
outcomes of peers of peers ($W^{2}y$) instrument for peer outcomes
($Wy$), rendering the model identified on network structure;
\textcite{bramoulle2020peer} and \textcite{boucher2024general}
provide the authoritative survey and the current theoretical
frontier of this lineage. In finance, \textcite{leary2014peer}
identify peer effects in corporate financial policy using
idiosyncratic peer shocks as own-excluded instruments---the classic
template for leave-self-out peer instruments---while
\textcite{grieser2022network} treat the specification of the network
weight matrix itself as the source of identification in a modern
spatial-econometrics benchmark. The recent instrument literature
further widens the admissible instrument families: granular
instrumental variables \parencite{gabaix2024granular} and the
practical shift-share guide \parencite{borusyak2025practical}
underpin the identification claims of the geographic leave-one-out
and generalized Bartik instruments used in our cross-family
over-identification test. Our $W^{2}\cdot\text{tone}$ instrument and
cross-family over-identification design sit squarely in this
lineage.

\subsection{Brokerage, Structural Holes, and Centrality Measurement}

The information-advantage construct of brokerage was established by
the structural-hole theory of \textcite{burt1992structural} and
received canonical empirical support in \textcite{burt2004structural}:
actors spanning structural holes access more heterogeneous
information and earn intermediation rents. On the measurement side,
\textcite{freeman1978centrality} laid the conceptual foundations of
degree, betweenness, and closeness centrality, and
\textcite{bonacich1972factoring} is the original source of
eigenvector centrality; \textcite{gould1989structures} formalized
brokerage into five roles (coordinator, itinerant broker,
gatekeeper, representative, liaison), which our panel's
\code{gf\_*} columns implement directly (gatekeeper and
representative collapse into a single column under the undirected
network), and
\textcite{zador2022weighted} extend the Gould--Fernandez measure to
weighted networks. Two-mode centrality on the media--bank bipartite
graph follows \textcite{borgatti1997network}. In finance,
\textcite{han2024brokerage} anchors the ``institutional brokerage
network'' construct at the top-journal level---we emphasize that we
cite it for the construct and its interpretation, not as an
algorithmic source---and the meta-analysis of
\textcite{nezami2025network} provides a cross-study benchmark for
the centrality--performance relation. Our preregistration of Freeman
betweenness as the primary centrality, with Bonacich eigenvector and
Burt constraint as secondary and structural-hole contrasts, draws on
exactly this construct--algorithm dual lineage.

\subsection{Financial Network Contagion and Systemic Risk}

The survey of \textcite{jackson2021systemic} establishes the
theoretical lineage of financial network contagion: centrally
positioned banks are both amplifiers and receivers of shocks, and
network structure determines whether local shocks escalate into
systemic events. \textcite{duarte2021firesale} aggregate bank-level
fire-sale spillover exposures into a forward-looking systemic-risk
index, providing the top-journal template for the aggregation logic
of our \code{brokerage\_fire\_sale} measure.
\textcite{yu2024centrality} explicitly connect network centrality,
information diffusion, and asset pricing, supporting our moderation
design in which information-hub positions amplify spillover
transmission. In the Chinese banking context,
\textcite{fang2024media} show that negative media sentiment raises
banks' funding costs and systemic risk through deposit instability,
constituting the direct mechanism anchor of our
``peer sentiment $\to$ performance'' transmission chain.

\subsection{Media, Banks, and the Chinese Institutional Context}

\textcite{you2018power} show that the state/market dual structure of
the Chinese media market generates systematic divergence in the tone
of coverage of the same firm, legitimizing the identifiable tone
variation in Chinese bank news corpora. \textcite{cookson2026social}
provide the most recent evidence that bank-level media exposure
amplifies run risk, anchoring the causal narrative that bank news
sentiment spills over to peers through network channels.
\textcite{xu2024news} build journalist social networks on financial
news corpora and explain tone slant with network positions---a
paradigm highly isomorphic to our ``news text $\to$ network position
$\to$ tone transmission'' design.

\subsection{Positioning of This Paper}

Three gaps remain. First, the peer-effects identification literature
has rarely used \emph{media co-occurrence}---an observable, massively
scalable physical event---as the network-generating mechanism.
Second, the structural-hole/brokerage literature concentrates on
individuals and innovation outcomes, with little causal evidence in
the context of bank performance and sentiment contagion. Third, the
financial contagion literature relies mostly on interbank
balance-sheet exposures or market returns; evidence on the
information-layer contagion channel---especially its imprint on
annual accounting performance---is scarce. With a corpus of tens of
millions of Chinese articles, second-order spillover instruments,
and a preregistered centrality discipline, this paper addresses all
three gaps simultaneously.

\section{Theory and Hypotheses}\label{sec:theory}

This section derives testable hypotheses from two theoretical
threads: information contagion and brokerage position. We state
upfront that each item below is a \emph{theoretical prediction}, not
an obtained empirical result; all coefficients and significance
levels await the production-panel regressions.

\textbf{Peer sentiment spillovers.} Media sentiment shapes the
beliefs of depositors and counterparties. Negative peer sentiment
depresses evaluations of similar banks through category-based
contagion, and converts belief shocks into real funding costs and
business contraction through common exposures and interbank linkages
\parencite{fang2024media,jackson2021systemic}. Conversely, improving
peer sentiment lifts the focal bank's operating environment through
the same channels. Hence:

\textbf{H1 (main identification, confirmatory).} First-order peer
tone \code{peer\_tone\_lag1\_full} (network 2SLS instrumented by
\code{peer\_tone\_2nd\_lag1\_full}) has a positive effect on ROA:
improving peer sentiment raises own performance; equivalently,
negative peer sentiment depresses ROA through information contagion.
Theoretical prediction: $\beta > 0$.

\textbf{Amplification by hub positions.} Information-diffusion
models predict that nodes in eigenvector-central hub positions
receive and retransmit more information flow
\parencite{bonacich1972factoring,yu2024centrality}; for banks on the
receiving end of sentiment shocks, hub positions imply a larger
exposure dose. Hence:

\textbf{H2 (moderation, exploratory family).} The interaction
\code{pt\_x\_brkr} $=$ \code{peer\_tone\_lag1\_full} $\times$
L.\code{brokerage\_eigen\_cent} carries a coefficient of the same
sign as the H1 main effect: information-hub positions amplify
spillover transmission. Estimation is interaction 2SLS
(\code{ivreghdfe}) with the interaction instrumented by
$W^{2}\cdot\text{tone}\times$ L.cent---not OLS
\parencite{brambor2006understanding}.

\textbf{The double edge of brokerage.} Burt's structural-hole theory
implies that community-spanning brokerage positions bring both
information advantages and a wider exposure surface
\parencite{burt1992structural,burt2004structural}; in a sentiment
contagion setting, banks bridging multiple communities are exposed to
sentiment shocks from all of them. Hence:

\textbf{H3 (heterogeneity, exploratory family).} The absolute
spillover effect is larger in the hub group defined by within-year
deciles (\code{bank\_is\_hub}=1) and in the structural-hole-rich
group (low \code{bank\_constraint}); between-group differences are
judged by the interaction $t$-test in a pooled interaction 2SLS, not
by side-by-side column comparison. This is a theoretical conjecture,
not a result.

\textbf{Fire-sale-style spillovers.} Industry-level negative shocks
aggregate onto individual banks through common exposures and
brokerage positions \parencite{duarte2021firesale,han2024brokerage}.
Hence:

\textbf{H4 (mechanism, exploratory appendix).} The lagged industry
negative shock \code{industry\_neg\_shock\_lag1} has a negative
effect on ROA, and banks with high \code{brokerage\_fire\_sale}
exposure (more central brokerage positions) suffer deeper losses.
The contemporaneous \code{industry\_neg\_shock} is exactly collinear
with the own negative rate under year fixed effects and is therefore
banned; only the one-year lag is used. Theoretical prediction, not a
result.

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Sample and Data}
The sample spans 2007--2024 ($T=18$) and covers 110 Chinese banks.
The raw-corpus census (the full recursive count \emph{before}
filtering and deduplication) comprises 183 deduplicated source
files, 18{,}198{,}978 media reports (2007-01-01 to 2025-12-31), and
3{,}740 media outlets; newspaper-type reports account for 92.93\%
of all rows (16{,}912{,}361 rows) and are retained, while other
types (wire services, magazines, government bulletins, web pages,
etc.) are dropped row-wise; exact-unique rows number 18{,}177{,}370
(duplication rate 0.12\%). After newspaper-type filtering,
deduplication, and restriction to 2007--2024, the production target
corpus is roughly 17 million articles; the bank--FinTech
co-occurrence hard filter (Section~\ref{subsec:network})
subsequently shrinks the actual regression corpus substantially
further (retained article count \NoteThis{pending
production-machine backfill}). The two accounting frames must not
be mixed: the census frame serves the data appendix, whereas the
(much smaller) post-hard-filter frame is the regression corpus. The final bank--year
panel size is \NoteThis{pending production-machine backfill
(paper\_spec target: $\geq 3{,}000$ bank--year observations)}.%
\footnote{The development snapshot covers only 2017--2018 (v3.3.0
baseline $=84$ banks $\times$ 2 years $=168$ observations; a
different accounting frame from the 110 banks in the main text) and
is used solely for pipeline integration; it generates no result in
the main text.}
All observations are processed by the \code{news\_process} pipeline;
see \code{papers\_assets/V6\_METHODOLOGY\_CHANGES.md}.

\subsection{Tone Annotation (F1: Three-Family LLM Validation)}
We employ three independent LLM families for sentiment annotation:
\code{gpt-5.4-nano} (OpenAI), \code{gemini-3.1-flash-lite}
(Google), and \code{deepseek-v4-flash} (DeepSeek). The OpenAI and
Google models are routed through yunwu.ai (\code{/v1/chat/completions},
OpenAI-compatible); DeepSeek is called directly at
\code{api.deepseek.com}. All calls use \code{temperature=0} and
\code{seed=42} with a byte-frozen prompt (version v8). Inter-family agreement reported as
Cohen's $\kappa$ and Krippendorff's $\alpha$; majority vote with ties marked
\code{undefined} (not \code{neutral}).

\subsection{Article-Level Coding (F2: Decontamination)}
Articles where all three families fail or split three ways are coded
\emph{missing}, not \code{neutral}. Bank--FinTech relevance failing
majority verification is also missing. This eliminates the 81.7\,\%
contamination in pre-v6 ``neutral'' labels.

Article retention is gated on the full-text track only
(\code{llm\_verified\_full}); the entity-context (\code{ctx}) and
same-sentence co-occurrence (\code{ss}) tracks are conditional-on-full
robustness tracks whose row universe is a subset of the full-track
retained set. Relative to earlier any-track-verified vintages, this
stricter gate mechanically shrinks \code{n\_articles} and derived
shares (\code{fintech\_share}, \code{attention\_pct})---a
definitional, conservative level shift, not a data error.

\subsection{Zero-Observation Semantics (F3: NaN Main Path)}
For bank--years with \code{n\_articles=0}, similarity-based features
(\code{auth\_sim\_*}, \code{benefit\_*}, \code{article\_sim\_*}),
ratios (\code{token\_adopt\_*}, \code{hidden\_adopt\_*},
\code{subst\_adopt\_*}), and \code{fintech\_intensity\_mean} are coded
as \code{NaN}. Counts remain at 0. Listwise deletion follows Allison
(2001) / Heckman (1979).

\subsection{Bartik IV Pruning (F4)}
Of approximately 30 candidate Bartik instruments, 28 are removed in v6
for violating Goldsmith-Pinkham \emph{et~al.}\ (2020, \emph{AER})
exogeneity. Retained: \code{bartik\_type\_iv\_full} and
\code{prov\_leave1out\_tone\_full}.

\subsection{Embedding-Based Measures (Co-Primary Dual Track; F5)}
All embedding-derived features are computed under two co-primary
embedding models from independent vendors, reported side by side as
mutual robustness: Alibaba \code{Qwen3-Embedding-8B} (4{,}096-d,
accessed via the SiliconFlow API; canonical unsuffixed columns) and
OpenAI \code{text-embedding-3-large} (3{,}072-d; \code{\_lg}-suffixed
columns), under strict column parity (each feature exists in both
tracks or in neither). Each track enters its own just-identified
specification; the two are never pooled into one over-identified
system. Earlier pipeline vintages used a Google Gemini embedding in
the canonical slot; as of pipeline v3.15.0 (June 2026) the canonical
slot is Alibaba \code{Qwen3-Embedding-8B}, with all embedding-derived
features regenerated under a controlled full re-embedding and
embedding-space semantic validity gated by a Spearman
rank-correlation check ($\rho \geq 0.80$). The sentiment-annotation
LLM ensemble (F1) is unaffected by this change: embedding models and
sentiment LLMs are disjoint pipeline components.

\subsection{Network Construction and Identification Design}\label{subsec:network}

\textbf{The co-occurrence network $W$.} Upstream, the corpus passes
a bank--FinTech co-occurrence hard filter (production setting: a
sentence-window radius of $\pm 1$ sentence with titles scanned
alongside the body, \code{sentence\_radius=1} +
\code{match\_in\_title=true}), retaining only articles in which the
target bank co-occurs with at least one FinTech term in proximity.
On the retained articles, an article mentioning banks $i$ and $j$
together records one co-occurrence; annual aggregation yields a
co-occurrence count matrix, which after row normalization gives the
weight matrix $W_{t}$ (zero diagonal, $\sum_{j} w_{ij,t}=1$). As a
construction-criterion sensitivity design, the bank--bank
co-occurrence criterion is rebuilt at three increasingly strict
settings, with all structural metrics recomputed under each:
same-article co-occurrence (unsuffixed, canonical), $\pm 1$
sentence window (\code{\_win1} suffix), and strict same-sentence
(\code{\_sent} suffix); titles are scanned as sentence zero. The
three criteria are specification-robustness parallels, each
just-identified; see Section \ref{sec:robust}.

\textbf{First- and second-order peer tone.} Let $\text{tone}_{j,t}$
denote bank $j$'s media tone in year $t$ (the F1 three-LLM
majority-vote consensus, full-text track). Then
\begin{equation}
\text{PeerTone}_{i,t} \equiv (W_{t}\,\text{tone})_{i}
= \sum_{j \neq i} w_{ij,t}\,\text{tone}_{j,t},
\qquad
\text{PeerTone}^{(2)}_{i,t} \equiv (W_{t}^{2}\,\text{tone})_{i}.
\label{eq:peertone}
\end{equation}
The panel columns are \code{peer\_tone\_lag1\_full} (lagged
first-order spillover, endogenous treatment) and
\code{peer\_tone\_2nd\_lag1\_full} (lagged second-order spillover,
instrument).

\textbf{2SLS equations.} The main identification is the following
network 2SLS (\code{ivreghdfe}):
\begin{equation}
\text{roa}_{i,t} = \beta\,\widehat{\text{PeerTone}}_{i,t-1}
 + X_{i,t}'\gamma + \mu_{i} + \lambda_{t} + \varepsilon_{i,t},
\label{eq:secondstage}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\text{PeerTone}_{i,t-1} = \pi\,\text{PeerTone}^{(2)}_{i,t-1}
 + X_{i,t}'\delta + \mu_{i} + \lambda_{t} + \nu_{i,t},
\label{eq:firststage}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_{i}$ and $\lambda_{t}$ are bank and year fixed effects,
$X_{i,t}$ is the control vector, and standard errors are clustered
at the province level (\code{prov\_id}). Exclusion follows from
intransitive triads: the sentiment of ``peers of peers'' can reach
the focal bank only through its direct peers
\parencite{bramoulle2009identification}. The current control set is
the coverage-proxy family (\code{ln\_news\_coverage},
\code{media\_hhi}, \code{bank\_scale\_ord}); bank financial controls
(size, leverage, capital adequacy, NPL ratio, deposit growth) and
macro controls (GDP growth, M2 growth) will be added once the
financial panel is merged.

\textbf{Centrality: preregistration discipline.} We strictly
separate the network-position measurement layer from the spillover
engine: the identification engine is the
$W\cdot\text{tone}$/$W^{2}\cdot\text{tone}$ spectral lags of
equations (\ref{eq:secondstage})--(\ref{eq:firststage});
centralities are \emph{not} spillover engines but carriers of
explanation, moderation, and heterogeneity. To preempt
``22-pick-3'' post-hoc selection, we preregister: the primary
centrality is Freeman betweenness \code{bank\_between\_cent}
\parencite{freeman1978centrality}---the only centrality that
directly quantifies cross-community information intermediation;
the secondary centrality is Bonacich eigenvector
\code{brokerage\_eigen\_cent} \parencite{bonacich1972factoring},
as a bipartite information-hub contrast
\parencite{borgatti1997network,han2024brokerage}; the
structural-hole contrast is Burt's constraint
\code{bank\_constraint} \parencite{burt1992structural}. The
remaining 19 centralities are demoted wholesale to an exploratory
appendix set, and any appendix use reports Romano--Wolf familywise
error control \parencite{romano2005stepwise}.

\textbf{Five roles of network position.} (i) \emph{Explanatory}
(\code{bank\_between\_cent} entering \code{reghdfe} directly):
network position is endogenous to media coverage, so these results
are read as conditional associations only, with no causal claim.
(ii) \emph{Mediator}: exploratory appendix, framed as an
association decomposition rather than causal mediation, always
accompanied by the $\rho$-sensitivity analysis of
\textcite{imai2010identification}. (iii) \emph{Instrument}: the
cross-family over-identification test (co-occurrence-family $W^{2}$
+ geography-family within-province leave-one-out); see Section
\ref{sec:robust}. (iv) \emph{Moderator}: interaction 2SLS (H2).
(v) \emph{Heterogeneity}: within-year decile grouping (H3).

\textbf{Missing-data convention and sample disclosure.} The
centrality family carries two distinct missing conventions:
\code{bank\_*\_cent} defaults to 0.0 for off-network banks, whereas
\code{brokerage\_eigen\_cent} defaults to \code{NaN}; using both in
one regression induces listwise sample drift. All templates
therefore adopt the unified ``zero-fill off-network + explicit
per-table $N$ disclosure'' convention
(\code{zero\_fill\_offnetwork\_disclose\_N}), and every regression
table reports observation counts column by column.

\subsection{Inference Plan}\label{subsec:inference}

\textbf{Clustering and small-$G$ correction.} Baseline standard
errors are clustered at the province level (\code{prov\_id})
\parencite{cameron2015practitioner}; since $G \approx 31 < 50$ is a
small-cluster setting, all main tables report wild cluster bootstrap
$p$-values in parallel (\code{boottest},
\code{bootcluster(prov\_id)}, \code{reps(9999)};
\parencite{roodman2019fast}). Two-way clustering (bank $\times$
year) is reported as a contrast in the robustness tables.

\textbf{Weak-instrument rule.} Every IV table reports the
Kleibergen--Paap rk Wald $F$; whenever it falls below the
Stock--Yogo 10\% critical value (16.38 for one instrument, 19.93
for two), inference switches to Anderson--Rubin weak-instrument-robust
confidence intervals (\code{weakiv}; \parencite{andrews2019weak}),
with AR-CI side outputs attached to every IV table.

\textbf{Confirmatory versus exploratory discipline.} The paper
follows a single-preregistered-headline discipline: there is exactly
one confirmatory test---the network 2SLS of equation
(\ref{eq:secondstage}), \code{peer\_tone\_lag1\_full} $\to$
\code{roa} (Table 2). The centrality battery, the five network-role
analyses, and the policy shocks all belong to exploratory families
whose $p$-values are interpreted only after familywise error control
\parencite{romano2005stepwise}. Multi-measure ladders (the three
text tracks, the dual embeddings, the three LLM vendors, the three
construction criteria, etc.) are parallel ``mutual robustness, no
primacy'' measures: each rung enters its own just-identified
specification, and rungs of the same family are never pooled into
one over-identified system for a Hansen $J$ test (common-source
contamination renders $J$ powerless); the single deliberate
exception is the cross-family instrument test of Section
\ref{sec:robust}.

\section{Data}\label{sec:data}

Table 1 reports variable definitions and descriptive statistics in
four families:
(i) the \emph{peer-spillover family}: the first-order spillover
\code{peer\_tone\_lag1\_full} and its ctx/ss twins
(\code{peer\_tone\_lag1\_ctx}, \code{peer\_tone\_lag1\_ss}), and the
instrument \code{peer\_tone\_2nd\_lag1\_full} with its track twins;
(ii) the \emph{network-position family}: the three preregistered
centralities \code{bank\_between\_cent},
\code{brokerage\_eigen\_cent}, \code{bank\_constraint}, and the hub
indicator \code{bank\_is\_hub} (within-year deciles);
(iii) the \emph{outcome family}: the primary outcome \code{roa} and

\section{Robustness}\label{sec:robust}

We first state the organizing principle of this section: within each
ladder below, the rungs are parallel ``mutual robustness, no
primacy'' measures, and each rung enters its own
\emph{just-identified} specification; rungs of the same family (any
two text tracks, the two embedding slots, any two LLM vendors, any
two construction criteria) are never pooled into one over-identified
system for a Hansen $J$ test---common-source measurement
contamination is highly correlated within family and renders $J$
powerless against invalid instruments. The single deliberate
exception is the cross-family instrument test (item 7 below).

\textbf{(1) Three-track peer tone.} The headline treatment and
instrument are rebuilt on the entity-context and same-sentence
tracks: \code{peer\_tone\_lag1\_\{full,ctx,ss\}} each paired with
the same-track instrument
\code{peer\_tone\_2nd\_lag1\_\{full,ctx,ss\}}, three 2SLS reported
side by side. The retention gate is full-only, with ctx/ss as
conditional-on-full robustness tracks (F2); the three tracks are
never pooled into one Hansen $J$.

\textbf{(2) The construction-criterion ladder.} The bank--bank
co-occurrence criterion is rebuilt at three increasingly strict
settings (same-article/unsuffixed, $\pm 1$ sentence window
\code{\_win1}, strict same-sentence \code{\_sent}) and all 33
structural metric base names are recomputed (e.g.,
\code{bank\_between\_cent\{,\_win1,\_sent\}},
\code{bank\_constraint\{,\_win1,\_sent\}}); the main tables are
re-estimated criterion by criterion. The three criteria are
specification-robustness parallels, each just-identified; because
they are not competing headline measurements of a single construct,
no FWER template is assigned. Note that criterion suffixes and the
text-track suffixes are two orthogonal dimensions and must not be
conflated.

\textbf{(3) Spillover-quantity tracks and $W$-tracks.} The industry
negative shock \code{industry\_neg\_shock\{,\_ctx,\_ss\}} (and its
lag1) is built strictly track-by-track in both numerator and
denominator (no borrowing of the full-track denominator);
embedding-similarity-weighted peer tone
(\code{bank\_emb\_sim\_mean\_lag1\_\{ctx,ss\}} and the diagonal
track \code{emb\_peer\_tone\_diag\_lag1\_\{ctx,ss\}}) degrades
independently per $W$-track and is reported side by side; no tracks
are mixed into one Hansen $J$.

\textbf{(4) Co-primary dual embeddings.} All embedding-derived
network columns exist pairwise under embeddings from two independent
vendors (F5): the canonical unsuffixed slot
(\code{Qwen3-Embedding-8B}, 4{,}096-d) and the \code{\_lg} slot
(\code{text-embedding-3-large}, 3{,}072-d), under strict
both-or-neither column parity. The two slots are co-primary
measures, each entering its own just-identified specification;
embedding-derived $W$-track robustness results are reported slot by
slot.

\textbf{(5) Per-model LLM reconstruction.} Beyond the consensus
tone (\code{llm\_vote\_tone}), peer spillovers are reconstructed
vendor by vendor from the single-model tones
\code{\{gpt54nano,gemini31,deepseekv4\}\_tone\_full}, demonstrating
that results are not driven by any single vendor's annotation; the
three vendors are co-equal parallels with per-model independent
degradation, each just-identified; true familywise error control is
carried by the cross-model Romano--Wolf template
\parencite{romano2005stepwise}.

\textbf{(6) Cross-method tone substitutes.} Peer tone is
reconstructed from three Chinese financial BERT models (the signed
net tones of \code{finbert\_cn}, \code{fin\_sent\_zh},
\code{finbert2}) and six sentiment dictionaries; the dictionary
family includes linguistic-rule ablation variants and the
political-positive word-weight ladder $\{0.0, 0.5, 1.0\}$ of
\textcite{du2022language} (canonical weight 0.5; the three rungs are
mutual robustness, no primacy). The two ladders are orthogonal and
never crossed; cross-model coefficient comparisons are always made
after $z$-score standardization; all rungs are just-identified
parallels.

\textbf{(7) Cross-family over-identification (the only permitted
over-id).} Same-family tracks are never pooled into a Hansen $J$,
but the joint-instrument test across two \emph{independent
identification channels}---the co-occurrence family
$W^{2}\cdot\text{tone}$ (\code{peer\_tone\_2nd\_lag1\_full}) and the
geography family within-province leave-one-out tone
(\code{prov\_leave1out\_tone\_full})---is a deliberate exception
(Table 8, \code{ivreghdfe}): the measurement-error sources of the
two families are orthogonal, so the Hansen $J$ has genuine power,
breaking the untestability of exclusion in just-identified designs
\parencite{leary2014peer,goldsmith2020bartik,gabaix2024granular,borusyak2025practical}.

\textbf{(8) FWER discipline for the 22-centrality battery.} The
main text reports only the three preregistered centralities
(betweenness/eigenvector/constraint); the remaining 19 (degree,
strength, Katz, subgraph, current-flow betweenness, harmonic,
coreness, etc.) are demoted wholesale to an exploratory appendix
(Table 11), and any use reports
\code{wyoung}/\code{rwolf2}/sharpened-FDR corrected $p$-values
\parencite{romano2005stepwise}.

\textbf{(9) Inference-layer robustness.} Province-level clustering
($G \approx 31$) with wild cluster bootstrap in parallel on all
tables \parencite{roodman2019fast}; two-way clustering (bank
$\times$ year) contrast (Table 12 Panel B); Anderson--Rubin CIs
whenever instruments are weak \parencite{andrews2019weak}.

\textbf{(10) Exploratory policy-shock tests.} The DCEP/sandbox
event studies (Table 3) enter the robustness reading as
mechanism-level corroboration without altering the status of the
main identification.

\textbf{(11) Missing-convention sensitivity.} Stability of the
headline coefficient under the off-network zero-fill convention
(\code{zero\_fill\_offnetwork\_disclose\_N}) versus listwise
deletion, with per-table $N$ disclosed column by column.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:concl}

This paper constructs a bank--bank media co-occurrence network on a
corpus of tens of millions of Chinese bank/FinTech news articles,
instruments first-order peer tone with the second-order peer tone of
Bramoull\'e--Djebbari--Fortin intransitive triads, identifies the
contagion of peer sentiment through network structure into bank
performance, and examines brokerage position (betweenness,
eigenvector, structural-hole constraint) as explanatory, moderating,
and heterogeneity carriers. We find \NoteThis{summary of core
findings: direction and magnitude of the H1 main effect and the
evidence pattern for H2--H4, pending production regressions}.

Theoretically, the paper connects the information-advantage
construct of the structural-hole/brokerage literature with the
amplifier--receiver lineage of financial network contagion at the
media information layer: the ``double edge'' of brokerage
positions---information advantage coexisting with a wider exposure
surface---receives a testable formulation in the sentiment-contagion
setting. Methodologically, the paper demonstrates a preregistration
discipline for the age of abundant text measures: a single
confirmatory headline, familywise error control for exploratory
families, and the reporting rule of ``parallel just-identified rungs,
never same-family over-identification,'' of general reference value
for empirical finance research with increasingly rich text-based
measurement.

For policy, if peer sentiment contagion holds
(\NoteThis{result-dependent sentence placeholder}), banks occupying
central media-network positions constitute amplifier nodes of
sentiment contagion: macroprudential authorities can add banks'
media-network brokerage positions to systemic-risk monitoring
dashboards as a low-cost information-layer complement to traditional
balance-sheet interconnectedness measures
\parencite{jackson2021systemic,duarte2021firesale}, and the handling
of negative-sentiment events should prioritize the secondary
transmission risk of high-betweenness banks
\parencite{fang2024media,cookson2026social}.

Four limitations follow. First, annual frequency: accounting
performance is observed yearly, which cannot capture the
high-frequency dynamics of sentiment contagion nor connect to
market-based systemic-risk measures requiring daily returns
($\Delta$CoVaR and the like); a daily-frequency extension on the
listed-bank subsample is left for future work. Second, media
coverage endogeneity: which banks are covered, and which appear
together, is endogenous to editorial choices; fixed effects,
instruments, and policy shocks mitigate but cannot fully eliminate
residual selectivity. Third, the mapping from co-occurrence to real
peer relations (co-occurrence $\approx$ peers) is an external
validity assumption; we assess it with QAP/dyadic tests in the
shared appendix (\code{\_shared/A\_network\_robustness}), but the
co-occurrence network remains an information-layer projection of the
true business network. Fourth, single-market extrapolation: the
state/market dual structure of the Chinese media market
\parencite{you2018power} is both a favorable source of identifying
variation and a limit on extrapolation to other institutional
environments.
