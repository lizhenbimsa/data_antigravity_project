\begin{abstract}
\NoteThis{Final abstract pending; all result statements await production-panel regressions.}
Amid the FinTech shock, Chinese banks' digital-transformation
narratives have become strikingly homogeneous, leaving open whether
strategic differentiation still translates into franchise value. We
transplant the Hoberg--Phillips (2016) framework of endogenous
product differentiation to FinTech competition in Chinese banking:
using a bank-year corpus of FinTech media coverage, we measure each
bank's strategic-narrative distinctness with the Guzman--Li (2023)
N-nearest-neighbor text distance (\code{strat\_diff\_n5} as the
pre-specified primary measure), complemented by product-market
fluidity (Hoberg--Phillips--Prabhala 2014) and multiple alternative
text representations. Identification relies on a single
pre-specified confirmatory specification with bank and year fixed
effects and bank-clustered standard errors, supported by Bartik-type
shift-share instruments and Romano--Wolf familywise error control.
The theoretical predictions are that strategic distinctness raises
listed banks' franchise value (Tobin's Q, $\beta>0$) and lowers
funding costs through deposit pricing power ($\beta<0$). We find
\NoteThis{key result (production-panel T=18 estimates pending)}\@.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Over the past decade, FinTech has reshaped banking competition across
payments, lending, and wealth management
\parencite{berg2022fintechlending,parlour2022payments,vives2025lendercompetition},
and China is among the markets where this transformation has been most
dramatic \parencite{hau2024fintechcredit,cheng2020bankfintech}. Facing
entry pressure from technology platforms, Chinese commercial banks
announced ``digital transformation'' strategies almost in unison, and
narratives of retail transformation, open banking, and digital
middle-platforms converged across the industry. When every bank tells
a similar story, a classic industrial-organization question resurfaces:
can strategic differentiation still soften homogeneous competition,
accumulate market power, and ultimately show up as a franchise-value
premium \parencite{hoberg2016tnic,drechsler2026franchise}? Media
coverage offers a natural window on banks' strategic narratives: news
text records the direction, intensity, and wording of each bank's
FinTech positioning, and media coverage is itself a central
information intermediary in capital markets
\parencite{fang2009media,you2018pen}. We build a corpus of Chinese
bank--FinTech news spanning 2007--2024: the raw census (before
filtering and deduplication) counts 18{,}198{,}978 media reports from
3{,}740 outlets, of which newspaper-type reports account for
92.93\,\% of all rows and are retained; after the newspaper-type
screen and \emph{before} the bank--FinTech entity co-occurrence hard
filter, the production target corpus is roughly 17 million articles
(2007--2024) covering 110 banks over 18 years (T=18). The
co-occurrence hard filter drops rows, so the article count entering
the regression panel is far smaller than this target corpus.

Our research question is whether the \emph{textual distinctness} of a
bank's FinTech strategic narrative raises its \emph{franchise value}.
We transplant the endogenous product differentiation framework of
\textcite{hoberg2016tnic} to FinTech competition in banking and adopt
the N-nearest-neighbor text distance of \textcite{guzman2023strategy},
measuring at the bank-year level the average distance between each
bank's narrative and its N most similar peers (\code{strat\_diff\_n5};
N=5 with a TF-IDF character representation is the pre-specified
primary measure), with product-market fluidity
\parencite{hoberg2014fluidity}, industry-centroid distance, LDA topic
divergence, and embedding distance serving as mutually robust
alternative representations. The theoretical logic is that more
distinct banks face weaker homogeneous competition and lower
product-market threats, accumulate market power, and---among listed
banks---command a Tobin's Q premium; in the full sample,
differentiation lowers funding costs through deposit pricing power.

For identification we impose bank and year fixed effects, cluster
standard errors at the bank level, and pre-specify a single
confirmatory specification (\code{strat\_diff\_n5} $\to$ Tobin's Q,
one-sided prediction $\beta>0$), explicitly labeling all alternative
measures, tracks, and heterogeneity analyses as exploratory, with
familywise error rates controlled within pre-declared families by
Romano--Wolf step-down resampling
\parencite{romano2005stepwise,list2019multiplehypothesis}. Against the
classic endogeneity concern that media coverage and firm value are
jointly determined \parencite{gurun2012hype}, we deploy Bartik-type
shift-share instruments whose construction and diagnostics follow the
modern share-exogeneity \parencite{goldsmith2020bartik} and
shock-exogeneity
\parencite{borusyak2022shiftshare,borusyak2025shiftshareguide}
standards.

The paper makes four contributions. First, measurement: to our
knowledge this is the first implementation of N-nearest-neighbor
strategic distinctness on a Chinese bank-news corpus, delivering a
complete measurement family of five text representations (TF-IDF
character / TF-IDF word / LDA+JSD / embedding / industry centroid)
crossed with an N-neighbor ladder
(N$\in\{3,\dots,7,10,20,30,40,50\}$), extending the text-based
strategy measurement lineage
\parencite{guzman2023strategy,bellstam2021innovation,banker2024strategy,hoberg2025scope}.
Second, identification and inference discipline: explicit
confirmatory/exploratory boundaries, pre-registered FWER families,
and Bartik instruments remain rare in observational bank-panel work.
Third, evidence: we provide systematic Chinese evidence on the
relation between bank differentiation and franchise value in the
FinTech era, adding a competitive-strategy dimension to the deposit
franchise literature \parencite{drechsler2026franchise}. Fourth,
methodology: given that text-based conclusions are highly sensitive
to embedding-model choice \parencite{ganguli2024patenttext}, all
embedding-derived measures are computed under two co-primary
embedding models from independent vendors, reported side by side as
mutual robustness (Section F5).

This paper belongs to a series built on the same data pipeline, but
its object is orthogonal to its siblings: unlike the tone paper (P1)
we measure N-neighbor textual distinctness, not firm tone; unlike the
attention paper (P2) we measure distinctness, not exposure intensity;
we do not construct binary substantive-adoption labels (P3) and do
not distinguish innovation locus (P4); unlike benefit\_society's
single attitude projection we use multi-representation text
differences; unlike brokerage\_network's network positions we use
text-representation differences; and unlike llm\_peer's LLM semantic
peer instrument, our core is the N-neighbor text distance.

\section{Literature Review}\label{sec:lit}

\subsection{Text-Based Strategic Distinctness and Optimal Distinctiveness}
Two strands anchor our measurement construct. The first is the
text-based network industries (TNIC) lineage pioneered by
\textcite{hoberg2016tnic}, which characterizes endogenous product
differentiation via pairwise similarity of firm text, with
differentiation governing the intensity of competition a firm faces;
\textcite{hoberg2014fluidity} further define product-market fluidity
as the speed at which rivals' text moves toward a firm's
neighborhood, measuring product-market threats, and
\textcite{hoberg2025scope} extend the lineage to the scope and scale
of the 21st-century firm. The second is the founding-strategy
measurement of \textcite{guzman2023strategy}, which measures
strategic distinctness as the text distance between a firm and its N
nearest neighbors; our \code{strat\_diff\_n\{N\}} column family is
its bank-news implementation. Relatedly,
\textcite{bellstam2021innovation} measure corporate innovation from
analyst-report text and \textcite{banker2024strategy} characterize
strategy typology and cost structure textually. The optimal
distinctiveness literature supplies boundary conditions:
\textcite{haans2019distinctiveness} shows that the performance return
to distinctiveness varies with category homogeneity;
\textcite{su2024optimal} emphasize dynamic positioning between
competitive and institutional pressures; and
\textcite{arts2025technology} provide recent evidence that technology
differentiation relaxes product-market rivalry. These boundary
conditions map into our ownership, size, and network-hub
heterogeneity designs. The industry-centroid variant has an
additional Chinese-language antecedent referenced inside the pipeline
code (Xie et al., 2025, \emph{Economic Research}); that record awaits
CNKI verification, so our formal citations anchor both the centroid
and N-neighbor operationalizations to \textcite{hoberg2016tnic} and
\textcite{guzman2023strategy}.

\subsection{FinTech, Bank Competition, and Franchise Value}
How FinTech entry reshapes bank competition is a central question in
recent finance research: \textcite{berg2022fintechlending} survey the
rise of FinTech lending; \textcite{vives2025lendercompetition}
theorize the effect of information technology on lender competition;
and \textcite{parlour2022payments} analyze how FinTech competition for
payment flows erodes banks' information rents. In the Chinese
context, \textcite{hau2024fintechcredit} document FinTech credit
fostering entrepreneurial growth, and \textcite{cheng2020bankfintech}
construct a bank FinTech index from Chinese text and study its credit
risk consequences---the closest antecedent to our text-based
approach. On the outcome side, our
``differentiation $\to$ market power $\to$ franchise value''
narrative connects to the deposit franchise literature:
\textcite{drechsler2026franchise} show that the deposit franchise is
a core component of bank value whose stability rests on banks'
deposit pricing power---precisely the theoretical landing point of
our funding-cost channel.

\subsection{Text-as-Data Methods and Embedding-Model Sensitivity}
\textcite{gentzkow2019text} provide the general blueprint of
``text $\to$ numerical representation $\to$ regression input.''
\textcite{hanley2019dynamic} and \textcite{engle2020climatenews}
demonstrate time-varying exposure measures built from financial
filings and news text respectively; \textcite{fang2009media}
establish the classic media-coverage design in asset pricing, and
\textcite{you2018pen} characterize the institutional divide between
state-controlled and marketized media in China, providing contextual
legitimacy for our Chinese media corpus. At the embedding-era
frontier, \textcite{cong2024textualfactors} propose a scalable and
interpretable textual-factors framework; most critically,
\textcite{ganguli2024patenttext} systematically show that
text-similarity conclusions depend strongly on embedding-model
choice---a finding that directly motivates the co-primary dual-track
parity design (Qwen3-Embedding-8B $\perp$ text-embedding-3-large) in
Section F5.

\subsection{Identification Strategy and Inference Discipline}
Our instrumental-variable strategy rests on the shift-share (Bartik)
identification literature: \textcite{goldsmith2020bartik} develop the
share-exogeneity route and Rotemberg-weight diagnostics, while
\textcite{borusyak2022shiftshare} and
\textcite{borusyak2025shiftshareguide} develop the shock-exogeneity
route and practical guidance; together they support our design in
which each track's Bartik instrument enters its own just-identified
specification. For multiple-testing discipline,
\textcite{romano2005stepwise} supply the step-down FWER correction we
use, and \textcite{list2019multiplehypothesis} provide an operational
precedent for declaring confirmatory/exploratory boundaries. On media
endogeneity, the classic warning of \textcite{gurun2012hype} about
local media slant and firm value justifies our IV demand; clustered
inference follows \textcite{cameron2015practitioner}.

Taken together, the literature has not yet implemented
N-nearest-neighbor textual strategic distinctness in banking---least
of all in China, where FinTech competition is fiercest---nor linked
such a measure to franchise value under modern inference discipline.
This paper fills that gap.

\section{Theory and Hypotheses}\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Differentiation and Franchise Value}
In the endogenous product differentiation framework of
\textcite{hoberg2016tnic}, the more similar a firm's text is to its
rivals', the stronger the substitution threat it faces and the weaker
its pricing power; conversely, differentiation places the firm in a
sparser region of product space, softening homogeneous competition
and accumulating market power. Transplanted to banking: when most
banks push FinTech transformation with highly convergent narratives,
a bank whose strategic narrative is distinctly different from its
peers is more likely to occupy a differentiated business niche
(clientele, scenarios, technology route), and its market power
capitalizes into franchise value, priced as a valuation premium.
Franchise value is measurable by Tobin's Q among listed banks. We
therefore state a theoretical prediction (not a result):

\textbf{H1 (confirmatory).} Strategic distinctness raises bank
franchise value: the coefficient of \code{strat\_diff\_n5} on
Tobin's Q (implemented as winsorized \code{tobin\_q\_w}) is predicted
positive ($\beta>0$); listed-bank subsample, main table. This is the
paper's single pre-specified confirmatory specification.

\subsection{Differentiation and Funding Costs}
A micro-foundation of franchise value is deposit pricing power
\parencite{drechsler2026franchise}: banks facing weak homogeneous
competition need not bid up deposit rates. If differentiation indeed
softens competition, it should surface on the liability side as lower
funding costs. This channel is testable in the full sample (including
unlisted banks), and its predicted direction is opposite to the
tone--funding-cost channel in the companion tone paper (P1), making
the two mutually cross-validating.

\textbf{H2.} Strategic distinctness lowers funding costs: the
coefficient of \code{strat\_diff\_n5} on \code{funding\_cost} is
predicted negative ($\beta<0$); full-sample channel.

\subsection{Income Structure and Product-Market Threat Channels}
If differentiation is effective, two intermediate channels should be
observable. First, differentiated banks are more likely to build
unique positions in non-interest businesses (wealth management,
technology output, scenario finance), tilting their income structure
toward non-interest income. Second, by the product-market fluidity
logic \parencite{hoberg2014fluidity}, rivals move more slowly toward
a differentiated bank's product space, so it faces lower
product-market threats. Profitability (ROA) is an auxiliary channel.

\textbf{H3 (mechanism, exploratory).} \code{strat\_diff\_n5} is
predicted positive on \code{noninterest\_income\_share}, negative on
\code{strat\_fluidity}, and positive on \code{roa}. All are secondary
outcomes, labeled exploratory.

\subsection{Complementarity of Differentiation and Network Hub Position}
The value of differentiation may depend on a bank's position in the
interbank network: a bank that is both differentiated and a network
hub combines a unique niche with informational and business network
advantages---the strongest moat. However, the interaction between
network position and differentiation is endogenous and admits no
causal interpretation \parencite{brambor2006understanding}.

\textbf{H4 (exploratory, conditional association).}
\code{diff\_x\_btwn} (= \code{strat\_diff\_n5} $\times$
L.\code{bank\_between\_cent}) is predicted to show a positive
\emph{conditional association} with Tobin's Q; per the audit ruling
(STRATDIFF-MOD-1), this interaction is framed strictly as a
conditional association and never as causal.

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Sample and Data}
The bank--year panel covers approximately 164 bank--years
(2017--2018). All bank--year observations are processed by the
\code{news\_process} pipeline (Phase 9b baseline; see
\code{papers\_assets/V6\_METHODOLOGY\_CHANGES.md}).

\subsection{Tone Annotation (F1: Three-Family LLM Validation)}
We employ three independent LLM families for sentiment annotation:
\code{gpt-5.4-nano} (OpenAI), \code{gemini-3.1-flash-lite}
(Google), and \code{deepseek-v4-flash} (DeepSeek). The OpenAI and
Google models are routed through yunwu.ai (\code{/v1/chat/completions},
OpenAI-compatible); DeepSeek is called directly at
\code{api.deepseek.com}. All calls use \code{temperature=0} and
\code{seed=42} with a byte-frozen prompt (version v8). Inter-family agreement reported as
Cohen's $\kappa$ and Krippendorff's $\alpha$; majority vote with ties marked
\code{undefined} (not \code{neutral}).

\subsection{Article-Level Coding (F2: Decontamination)}
Articles where all three families fail or split three ways are coded
\emph{missing}, not \code{neutral}. Bank--FinTech relevance failing
majority verification is also missing. This eliminates the 81.7\,\%
contamination in pre-v6 ``neutral'' labels.

Article retention is gated on the full-text track only
(\code{llm\_verified\_full}); the entity-context (\code{ctx}) and
same-sentence co-occurrence (\code{ss}) tracks are conditional-on-full
robustness tracks whose row universe is a subset of the full-track
retained set. Relative to earlier any-track-verified vintages, this
stricter gate mechanically shrinks \code{n\_articles} and derived
shares (\code{fintech\_share}, \code{attention\_pct})---a
definitional, conservative level shift, not a data error.

\subsection{Zero-Observation Semantics (F3: NaN Main Path)}
For bank--years with \code{n\_articles=0}, similarity-based features
(\code{auth\_sim\_*}, \code{benefit\_*}, \code{article\_sim\_*}),
ratios (\code{token\_adopt\_*}, \code{hidden\_adopt\_*},
\code{subst\_adopt\_*}), and \code{fintech\_intensity\_mean} are coded
as \code{NaN}. Counts remain at 0. Listwise deletion follows Allison
(2001) / Heckman (1979).

\subsection{Bartik IV Pruning (F4)}
Of approximately 30 candidate Bartik instruments, 28 are removed in v6
for violating Goldsmith-Pinkham \emph{et~al.}\ (2020, \emph{AER})
exogeneity. Retained: \code{bartik\_type\_iv\_full} and
\code{prov\_leave1out\_tone\_full}.

\subsection{Embedding-Based Measures (Co-Primary Dual Track; F5)}
All embedding-derived features are computed under two co-primary
embedding models from independent vendors, reported side by side as
mutual robustness: Alibaba \code{Qwen3-Embedding-8B} (4{,}096-d,
accessed via the SiliconFlow API; canonical unsuffixed columns) and
OpenAI \code{text-embedding-3-large} (3{,}072-d; \code{\_lg}-suffixed
columns), under strict column parity (each feature exists in both
tracks or in neither). Each track enters its own just-identified
specification; the two are never pooled into one over-identified
system. Earlier pipeline vintages used a Google Gemini embedding in
the canonical slot; as of pipeline v3.15.0 (June 2026) the canonical
slot is Alibaba \code{Qwen3-Embedding-8B}, with all embedding-derived
features regenerated under a controlled full re-embedding and
embedding-space semantic validity gated by a Spearman
rank-correlation check ($\rho \geq 0.80$). The sentiment-annotation
LLM ensemble (F1) is unaffected by this change: embedding models and
sentiment LLMs are disjoint pipeline components.

\subsection{Constructing the Strategic Distinctness Measures}\label{sec:method-stratdiff}
The paper-specific treatment family \code{strat\_diff\_*} is
constructed at the bank-year level under five text representations,
with a common distinctness definition: given each bank's aggregated
FinTech news text vector in a year, bank $i$'s strategic distinctness
is the average distance to its $N$ nearest peer neighbors (the
N-nearest-neighbor niche distance, operationalized following
\textcite{guzman2023strategy}). The five representations
are: (i) TF-IDF character n-grams (canonical; the pre-specified
primary measure \code{strat\_diff\_n5} is the N=5 character variant);
(ii) TF-IDF words (jieba segmentation); (iii) LDA topic
distributions with Jensen--Shannon divergence as the distance;
(iv) embedding cosine distance (co-primary dual track, see F5);
(v) the industry-centroid method: the cosine distance between a
bank's text vector and the leave-one-out industry centroid
(\code{centroid\_diff\_*} family). Auxiliary measures include the
maximum similarity to the nearest neighbor (\code{strat\_sim\_max}),
within-bank text coherence (\code{coh\_tfidf\_c\_full}), and
product-market fluidity (\code{strat\_fluidity})---following the
fluidity-construction logic of \textcite{hoberg2014fluidity}, which captures
product-market threats via rivals' textual movement toward the
focal bank's neighborhood.

\subsection{The N-Neighbor Ladder and FWER Family Definitions}\label{sec:method-fwer}
There is no unique prior value of the neighborhood width N, so we
materialize the full ladder N$\in\{3,4,5,6,7,10,20,30,40,50\}$
(\code{strat\_diff\_n\{N\}}); the N variants are mutually robust with
no primacy ordering, each entering its own just-identified
specification. Multiple testing is corrected within pre-declared
families: the headline n\_neighbor family comprises all 10 N members
of the character representation; the centroid family comprises the 4
centroid variants (\code{centroid\_diff\_tfidf\_c},
\code{centroid\_diff\_tfidf\_w}, \code{centroid\_diff\_lda},
\code{centroid\_diff\_emb});
within each family we report Romano--Wolf step-down resampling
corrected $p$-values \parencite{romano2005stepwise}. Large-N columns
of the word/lda/emb/\code{\_lg} variants and of the ctx/ss tracks
serve only as point-estimate exploratory robustness and do not form
separate FWER families.

\subsection{Clustering and Inference Conventions}\label{sec:method-cluster}
Baseline standard errors are clustered one-way at the bank level (the
production panel has $G\approx110$ clusters, i.e., 110 banks, over
T=18); the listed-bank subsample of the main table has even fewer
clusters, backstopped by Cameron--Gelbach--Miller-style small-$G$
sensitivity checks; robustness checks compare two-way bank $\times$
year clustering \parencite{cameron2015practitioner}. There is exactly one
confirmatory specification (H1); all alternative measures, tracks,
and subsamples are labeled exploratory.

\subsection{Network Control Column and Missing-Value Conventions}\label{sec:method-network}
To separate the confound between ``textual strategic difference'' and
``network isolation,'' the baseline controls for the bank's Burt
structural constraint (\code{bank\_constraint}, network
embeddedness). Disclosure convention: bank-years with degree $<2$
are \code{NaN} in this column, and the listwise sample loss must be
reported in table notes (N-02); other centrality columns follow a
zero-fill convention for off-network banks with N disclosed. The
three-tier network co-occurrence criteria (same article, unsuffixed /
$\pm$1-sentence window \code{\_win1} / strict same sentence
\code{\_sent}) provide control-column substitution checks
(Section \ref{sec:robust}).

\subsection{Researcher--AI Collaboration Disclosure}\label{sec:method-ai}
Following the AI-use policies of the target journals, the author
discloses three classes of AI tools; all outputs were reviewed and
verified by the author, who bears full responsibility.

\emph{LLM sentiment annotation and routing.} Article-level sentiment
and relevance labels come from a majority-vote ensemble of three
independent-vendor LLMs (OpenAI \code{gpt-5.4-nano}, Google
\code{gemini-3.1-flash-lite}, DeepSeek \code{deepseek-v4-flash});
the OpenAI and Google models are routed through yunwu.ai's
OpenAI-compatible endpoint, while DeepSeek is called directly at
\code{api.deepseek.com}. All calls use \code{temperature=0},
\code{seed=42}, and byte-frozen prompt version v8; full invocation
logs and cache databases are preserved in the replication package,
enabling zero-cost offline reproduction.

\emph{Retention gating.} Whether an article counts as bank--FinTech
coverage is determined solely by full-track verification
(\code{llm\_verified\_full}); the \code{ctx} and \code{ss} tracks are
conditional-on-full robustness tracks (see F2). The mechanical level
shift of this gate (shrinking \code{n\_articles} and derived shares)
is a definitional consequence, disclosed in F2.

\emph{Dual embeddings and vendor swap.} All embedding-derived
measures are computed in parallel under Alibaba
\code{Qwen3-Embedding-8B} (4{,}096-d, canonical) and OpenAI
\code{text-embedding-3-large} (3{,}072-d, \code{\_lg}) under strict
column parity as co-primary mutual robustness; the canonical slot's
vendor dimension underwent a Google $\to$ Alibaba Qwen swap (pipeline
v3.15.0, June 2026), after which all embedding-derived features were
regenerated in a controlled full re-embedding and passed the Spearman
$\rho\geq0.80$ semantic-validity gate (see F5).

\section{Main Results}\label{sec:main}

The confirmatory specification is a two-way fixed-effects regression
on the listed-bank subsample:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
\mathit{TobinQ}^{w}_{it}
  = \beta\,\mathit{StratDiff}^{(n5)}_{it}
  + \boldsymbol{\gamma}'\mathbf{X}_{it}
  + \alpha_i + \lambda_t + \varepsilon_{it},
\end{equation}
where $\mathit{TobinQ}^{w}_{it}$ is winsorized Tobin's Q,
$\mathit{StratDiff}^{(n5)}_{it}$ is the N=5 TF-IDF character
distinctness, $\mathbf{X}_{it}$ collects bank-level and macro
controls, and $\alpha_i$, $\lambda_t$ are bank and year fixed
effects; standard errors are clustered at the bank level. The Stata

\section{Mechanism}\label{sec:mech}

If differentiation operates through market power, two channels should
be observable. The first is the deposit-pricing channel:
differentiated banks face weaker homogeneous competition, need not
bid up deposit rates, and thus enjoy lower funding costs (H2); this
channel's direction is opposite to the tone channel in the companion
tone paper (P1), making the two mutually cross-validating---tone
captures the attitudinal dimension of media evaluation, distinctness
the spatial dimension of strategic positioning, and if both show
opposite yet theory-consistent signs on funding cost, they jointly
rule out the common confound of mechanical coverage-volume effects.
The second comprises the product-market threat and income structure
channels (H3): Table 3 re-estimates Eq.~(\ref{eq:main}) with
\code{strat\_fluidity} (predicted negative),
\code{noninterest\_income\_share} (predicted positive), and
\code{roa} (predicted positive) as outcomes, all labeled exploratory.
Interpretively, a significantly negative fluidity coefficient
together with a significantly positive non-interest income share
supports the chain of ``differentiation occupies a sparse niche and
uniquifies the income structure.''

\section{Heterogeneity}\label{sec:het}

Following the boundary conditions of the optimal distinctiveness
literature \parencite{haans2019distinctiveness,su2024optimal},
Tables 4 and 8 will report four subsample splits: state-owned (SOE)
vs.\ non-SOE, network hub vs.\ non-hub, size groups, and bank-type
groups; all labeled exploratory. Theoretically, large state-owned
banks face stronger institutional pressure and narrower
differentiation latitude, so the return to distinctness may
concentrate among joint-stock and city commercial banks; this
expectation is exploratory and not a pre-specified test.

For moderation, per H4 we estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mod}
\mathit{TobinQ}^{w}_{it}
  = \beta_1\,\mathit{StratDiff}^{(n5)}_{it}
  + \beta_2\,\mathit{StratDiff}^{(n5)}_{it}\times
    L.\mathit{BankBetween}_{i,t-1}
  + \beta_3\,L.\mathit{BankBetween}_{i,t-1}
  + \boldsymbol{\gamma}'\mathbf{X}_{it} + \alpha_i + \lambda_t

\section{Robustness}\label{sec:robust}

All robustness dimensions in this section share one inference
discipline: within each ladder the members are \emph{mutually robust
with no primacy ordering}, each entering its own just-identified
specification, reported side by side; members of any ladder are
\emph{never} pooled into a single over-identified system (e.g., by
entering several highly correlated twin measures or several
instruments into one Hansen J test)---highly correlated twins carry
no independent information, and assembling them into over-identified
tests only manufactures spurious over-identification power.

\paragraph{N-neighbor sensitivity ladder (headline FWER family).}
Table 5a runs Eq.~(\ref{eq:main}) separately for each of
\code{strat\_diff\_n\{3,4,5,6,7,10,20,30,40,50\}} (TF-IDF character,
full track), reporting Romano--Wolf corrected $p$-values within the
family \parencite{romano2005stepwise}. Two statistical disclosures:
first, the 2026-07 expansion grew the family from 5 members (n3--n7)
to 10 (adding n10--n50); the Romano--Wolf correction becomes
\emph{monotonically stricter} as the family grows, mechanically
raising the corrected $p$-values of the pre-existing members
n3--n7 (including n5)---a definitional consequence of family
expansion that referees should interpret under the corrected scope.
Second, \code{strat\_diff\_n5} plays a dual role: it is both the
\emph{uncorrected single pre-specified hypothesis} of the
confirmatory specification (Section \ref{sec:main}) and an ordinary
member of this family subject to RW correction. The two scopes are
orthogonal and serve different purposes---the former tests a single
pre-registered theoretical hypothesis, the latter answers the family
question of whether the choice of N drives the conclusion---so the
same measure appearing in both places is not double-counting.
Large-N columns of the word/lda/emb/\code{\_lg} variants and the
ctx/ss tracks serve only as point-estimate exploratory robustness
and form no separate FWER family.

\paragraph{Representation-method family.}
The five text representations (TF-IDF character / TF-IDF word /
LDA+JSD / embedding / centroid) are each run separately and never
enter the same right-hand side simultaneously; the headline stays
character-based. The centroid family
(\code{centroid\_diff\_tfidf\_c} / \code{centroid\_diff\_tfidf\_w} /
\code{centroid\_diff\_lda} / \code{centroid\_diff\_emb}) is the second
pre-declared FWER family (4 members) with RW-corrected $p$-values.
The auxiliary measures \code{strat\_sim\_max} and
\code{coh\_tfidf\_c\_full} are each run alone.

\paragraph{Three text tracks full/ctx/ss.}
Retention gating is full-only; the \code{ctx} and \code{ss} tracks
are conditional-on-full robustness tracks whose row universe is a
subset of the full-track retained set (F2).
\code{strat\_diff\_n5\_ctx} and \code{strat\_diff\_n5\_ss} each run
Eq.~(\ref{eq:main}) alone; the three tracks are mutually robust,
highly correlated twins rather than independent hypotheses, and a
full$\times$ctx$\times$ss composite Hansen J is strictly prohibited.

\paragraph{Co-primary dual embeddings.}
The embedding-method column family is reported in strict column
parity under Qwen3-Embedding-8B (canonical) and
text-embedding-3-large (\code{\_lg}) (F5); each track enters its own
just-identified specification and the two are never pooled into one
over-identified system. The scholarly necessity:
\textcite{ganguli2024patenttext} show text-similarity conclusions
depend strongly on embedding-model choice; consistent signs across
independent vendors establish robustness to that choice.

\paragraph{Bartik IV three-track mirror and diagnostics.}
IV specifications are mirrored by track:
\code{bartik\_type\_iv\_full} (primary) / \code{bartik\_type\_iv\_ctx}
(fallback) / \code{bartik\_type\_iv\_ss} (ss mirror); each track's
endogenous variable is paired with its own-track instrument, each
just identified; mechanical \code{\_ctx}$\to$\code{\_ss} swaps into
over-identified systems are prohibited. Roughly 30 candidate
instruments were pruned to 2 under the
\textcite{goldsmith2020bartik} exogeneity standard (F4). Table A3
reports Rotemberg weights and the KP rk F.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:concl}

This paper transplants the endogenous product differentiation
framework to FinTech competition in Chinese banking, implements the
N-nearest-neighbor textual strategic distinctness measure on a
Chinese bank-news corpus for the first time, and tests the
``differentiation $\to$ market power $\to$ franchise value''
proposition under the inference discipline of a single pre-specified
confirmatory specification, Bartik instruments, and Romano--Wolf
familywise error control. We find \NoteThis{summary of key results
(production-panel estimates pending)}\@.

Theoretically, the paper brings the optimal distinctiveness and
text-based industrial organization literatures into the banking
context: if differentiation indeed accompanies a valuation premium
and lower funding costs, then even in a heavily regulated,
homogeneous industry, strategic-narrative differentiation remains a
viable source of franchise value. For policy, the results
\NoteThis{conditional on signs and magnitudes} would suggest that
regulators encouraging differentiated positioning---rather than a
homogeneous digital arms race---help preserve bank franchise value
and systemic stability; for bank management, niche selection is a
strategic lever in the FinTech era.

Three limitations apply. First, the panel is annual, so intra-year
strategic dynamics and short-run market reactions are not captured.
Second, media coverage is itself endogenous: coverage scope reflects
outlet selection and bank public relations \parencite{gurun2012hype};
although we mitigate this with Bartik and leave-one-out instruments,
exclusion restrictions are untestable and causal interpretation
warrants caution. Third, the sample covers the single Chinese market,
whose institutional environment (state ownership dominance, heavy
regulation, platform-economy structure) is distinctive, so
extrapolation to other banking systems should be cautious. The
financial-panel enrichment and the full T=18 production regressions
are forthcoming, at which point all result-dependent statements will
be updated.
